## SECTION 4: EXECUTIVE ABSTRACT

We propose to create a documented research resource about parent-observed sudden developmental changes associated with a child's later autism presentation. The study will preserve parent accounts, timing estimates, uncertainty, supporting evidence and reviewer disagreement, and publish safely coded data and redacted narratives for reuse. It is not designed to estimate population incidence, vaccinated-versus-unvaccinated autism risk, vaccine-attributable risk or a causal effect.

Recruitment has three stages. First, a participating autism membership organization, or a diagnosis/treatment clinic if membership recruitment is unavailable, administers a brief neutral contact-interest screen for its own records. The screen asks whether a possible sudden-onset child is present and whether the parent wants an invitation and authorizes sharing an email with the study team. The partner retains individual screening answers; the study receives authorized emails, administrative source/batch/permission attestations and available aggregate recruitment counts. The partner's regulatory role and privacy/retention arrangements require review.

Second, every authorized possible-case referral may complete Survey 2, regardless of date confidence or perceived record availability. After research consent and the required legal authority are established, the first substantive question collects an unprompted account of the 15 days before the first clear developmental change through that day. The account is saved and locked before named candidate events are disclosed. Neutral timing, date-confidence, uncertain-functioning and records-confidence questions follow. An additional disclosure then explains questions about illness, medications, medical visits and vaccination. Only participants affirming continuation receive event-specific questions. Best estimates, unknowns and partial responses remain separately labeled.

Third, the study progressively requests existing supporting materials from consenting respondents in four records-confidence groups, beginning with the most confident. The focused records cohort concerns first-noticed changes at 6-24 completed months. The target is 100 administratively complete documented-visit unique-child submissions; 25 fully validated cases is the project lead's minimum for proceeding if recruitment falls short. The exact validated-count definition remains TBD and will be fixed before enrollment. The target, scientific eligibility, compensation and public-release eligibility are distinct.

Three reviewers independently assess each case in one pass using all available information. They are blinded to one another's judgments, not to exposure. Field-level exact agreement by at least two reviewers supports accepted values; original reports, uncertainty and disagreements are retained. The resource includes one parent-first-notice ONSET, age at ONSET, vaccination-to-ONSET lag, normal-day counts within that window and descriptive reference-date lags where inputs support them.

The intended study platform is IPAK EDU, with deployment and security details TBD. Authorized study data and uploaded evidence will be retained encrypted under restricted access for 20 years after closure, subject to approval and applicable requirements. Source files will not be public. Privacy-approved coded data and redacted narratives will be deposited on Zenodo independently of findings or journal acceptance, with controlled re-review of source evidence available under approved access arrangements. Exact staged consent, partial-response use, authority, retention and public-sharing terms are submitted for IRB determination.

## SECTION 8: METHODS

### 8.1 Design and participant population

This is an observational, English-language parent-report and existing-source-evidence resource. Adult respondents must understand the consent and have the appropriate authority for the information and records involved. A parent's autism report is sufficient for survey participation; a professional diagnosis document is not required. Survey 2 records professional diagnosis status, approximate date and clinician type when known, with documentary status distinguished from parent report.

All authorized possible-case referrals are offered Survey 2 during the approved recruitment window, including uncertain dates, low records-confidence and subsequently out-of-range reports. The focused records cohort uses ONSET at 6 through 24 completed months inclusive. The 25-month anniversary is outside this range. Current child/adult status is separate from onset age and requires the determinations in Section 9.

One child is selected per family: the youngest autistic child who appeared to develop normally and had an identifiable first clear change at 6-24 completed months; if no child fits or the parent is unsure, the youngest autistic child. This instruction precedes the narrative but is not a screening question. Records requests focus on eligible reports; other study-collected answers remain retained with their dispositions.

### 8.2 Operational phenotype and ONSET

The target presentation is apparent prior normal development followed by a clear, lasting loss or substantial reduction of an acquired skill, a clearly new marked behavior, or both, first noticeable to either parent on an identifiable date. New-behavior-only cases are labeled separately from conventional skill-loss regression. There is no seven-day cutoff and no requirement that the entire change occur overnight.

Persistence means a lasting change in the child's usual behavior or skills rather than a temporary acute symptom that resolves without that developmental change. Prior skill/behavior, direct observer, first change, subsequent course and competing explanations are collected. Prior developmental concerns are not erased: evidence incompatible with the previously normal description produces a negative or uncertain phenotype assessment with a reason. Fever, transient crying or illness alone does not establish the phenotype. We do not add an unagreed fixed persistence-duration threshold.

ONSET is the single date either parent first noticed the qualifying lasting change. It is not biological onset, an acute symptom's beginning, diagnosis date, vaccination date or a documentation date. Parent dates, best estimates, precision, plausible bounds, reviewer assessments and accepted values are separate. Exact recollection is not verification; approximate dates are not converted to invented midpoint days.

### 8.3 Three-stage collection sequence

**Stage 1 — partner contact-interest screen.** The partner asks two questions: whether a possible sudden-onset child is present, allowing Yes/No/Unsure, and interest/explicit authorization to share an email with the identified study team. No birth year, age/date, date confidence, records-confidence or candidate-event questions are asked. Refer authorized Yes/Unsure possible cases. The partner's individual answers remain with it under its disclosed policy. Only authorized emails and administrative transfer metadata reach the study; available aggregate attempted/delivered/response/referral counts are requested. Partner-owned screening does not itself establish exemption or nonengagement. Screen text and transfer agreements are submitted for review.

**Stage 2 — our research survey.** Every authorized possible-case referral receives an invitation without confidence-based selection. Research consent and approved authority precede substantive answers. A1 is the first substantive question: an unprompted description of baseline, each of the 15 days preceding first notice and first notice itself, including direct observations, who noticed and subsequent course. Named-event examples do not appear in invitations, titles, help or pre-disclosure questions. Parents can explicitly report inability to recall a day.

After A1 confirmation/server lock, A2 records first-notice date, precision/bounds and date confidence; A3 records the most recent earlier uncertain-functioning period and any full return to baseline, without a forced 15/90-day lookback; A4 obtains neutral records-confidence. Additional disclosure and affirmative continuation follow A1-A4 saving. Later structured questions collect birthdate without requiring proof, residence country at ONSET/selected vaccination, phenotype/diagnosis, prompted full-window chronology, medical/other events, visits, actual vaccine products, illness/recovery and normal-day information. Prompted answers cannot overwrite A1.

**Stage 3 — phased supporting-material requests.** Survey respondents may authorize later records contact. Request batches are selected by the original neutral A4 records-confidence, within the consented receipt-time reported phenotype/6-24-month pool. Only when a person's request phase opens are records-sharing authorization, active compensation/deadline and secure upload displayed. Finishing Survey 2 does not require completing a deferred records task. Requests and uploads are separate from survey access and completion.

### 8.4 Confidence groups, batch sizes and stopping

The four ordered groups are: definitely available now; probably available/know how to obtain; might be available/need to investigate; probably unavailable. Unsure/missing confidence is unclassified, not a fifth ranked group or a survey exclusion; clarify neutrally where possible. Date confidence does not control survey access or ranking within a records group.

Initially request materials from a random 100 eligible consenting candidates in the highest-confidence group, filling from subsequent groups if needed and capping at the available pool. Use a recorded seed, deterministic input ordering, archived pool snapshot/hash and versioned draw. At the close of a request batch's 14-day window, let I_m be its matured request count, C_m its administratively complete documented-visit unique-child yield, and C the corresponding total received. If C < 100 and C_m > 0, the next request count is `ceil(1.20 * (100-C) / (C_m/I_m))`. With zero/unestimable yield, use 100 or the remaining pool. Exhaust confidence groups in order. The request denominator is not the Survey 2 invitation denominator.

Selection staff use a minimized queue containing permission, original confidence and receipt-time reported eligibility, excluding exposure timing/status/products, causal beliefs, reviewer judgments and interim plots. Those fields cannot determine invitations, requests, batch sizes or payment. Stop opening new records-request batches once C reaches 100; retain and review already requested submissions beyond 100. Later scientific exclusion does not trigger replacement recruitment. Confirmed duplicates/clerical errors may amend administrative counts through an audit.

If yield is inadequate, approach up to three additional approved organizations or clinics sequentially, with separate funnels. Reaching 25 validated cases is not an automatic recruitment stop. The target is not a power claim. **Fully validated minimum-count definition, including no-anchor and approximate-date cases: TBD; no unanswered recommendation has been treated as an agreed rule.** Below 25, report feasibility shortfall without suppressing permitted data or withholding owed payments.

**Recruitment start/end dates, Survey 2 enrollment ceiling, overall duration, closure milestone and support commitments:** TBD. These must be fixed before deployment; confidence-free survey invitations remain subject to that approved window, not an indefinite obligation to enroll.

### 8.5 Narrow evidence request and date provenance

Request the most recent actual vaccination date/products record on or before ONSET within inclusive 0-90 calendar days. If no vaccination occurred in that window, request the most recent regular vaccine-free well-child visit in the same window if present. Request any existing dated onset-related material, including a message, diary/calendar, clinical note, lawfully recorded call or contemporaneous call note, photograph or video. No before-video, proof of birthdate or full vaccine history is required. Families with neither eligible visit may submit onset evidence and remain retained.

A vaccine record establishes administration at its supported precision, not necessarily that it was the most recent vaccination. Ask about later vaccination and preserve history/coverage uncertainty. A record with no vaccine mentioned does not automatically prove no shots at that visit or elsewhere in the window. Parent-reported lifetime nonvaccination remains labeled parent report unless separately supported. Optional additional documentary history can improve coverage without becoming a participation requirement.

For each item, preserve original bytes, receipt/checksum/provenance, creation/recording date, event date described and parent-claimed first-notice date with certainty/bounds. These dates may legitimately differ. A dated after-change video can establish documented presence, not automatically first notice. A call log alone does not document what was said. Keep originals restricted; derived viewing/redaction copies are labeled. Malware scanning and safe preview controls are TBD for platform confirmation.

Resolve material conflicts through one consolidated neutral clarification request, one day-7 reminder and a routine 14-day response window. Nonresponse remains unresolved; late evidence is auditable, not a silent overwrite of locked reports or review.

### 8.6 Independent review and acceptance

Each of Steve Kirsch, Karl Jablonowski and Brian Hooker reviews every candidate packet independently in one pass. All evidence, including vaccination information, is visible; other reviewers' judgments and interim group plots are not. Each assessment is timestamped and locked before sharing.

Record phenotype Yes/No/Uncertain, birthdate, first-notice date/precision/bounds/support, earliest documented post-change date, most recent eligible vaccination/products or vaccine-free well visit, coverage limitations, normal-day assessment and conflicts. Exact agreement by at least two reviewers accepts a field/status/bound. No averaging, clamping or favorable-case adjudication substitutes for unresolved agreement. Check accepted combinations for chronological consistency. Agreement does not establish verification; distinguish retrospective report, documented presence and contemporaneous first-notice support. Training/calibration and actual reviewer isolation are TBD for prelaunch verification.

### 8.7 Measures and data construction

**A:** ONSET minus the selected actual vaccination date within 0-90 days. Same-day dates yield A=0 but do not establish intraday order; collect before/after/unknown order. Without an eligible vaccination A is not applicable, not zero.

**B:** Total complete normal calendar days between vaccination and ONSET, including disconnected normal periods. Normal means observed pre-vaccination behavior-and-skill baseline. Include vaccination day only if normal throughout; exclude ONSET day and pre-vaccination days. Exact values satisfy 0 <= B <= A; A=0 implies B=0. Partial-day returns are separate. Unknown/unassessable days are not abnormal. When exact and reconciled, definitely normal N + definitely not complete-normal D + unknown U = A; U>0 supports B bounds N to N+U, not an exact B=N. Keep direct counts, timelines, raw B>A and discrepancies. B is not A minus fever duration. No eligible vaccination means B not applicable.

**C:** ONSET minus a constructed reference date. Calendar-quarter C uses the most recent January 1, April 1, July 1 or October 1 on/before ONSET, without an artificial 90-day cutoff; offsets can reach 91 days. Nominal-age C uses original birthdate plus 2/4/6/12/15/18 calendar months, selecting the most recent point on/before ONSET within 90 days. Clamp an absent day to month end; do not add age points, customize by birth year or infer products/actual doses. This fixed schedule-inspired benchmark and calendar quarter are descriptive references, not proven no-effect comparators.

Other fields include age and weekday at ONSET, vaccine-free well-visit lag, prompted symptom/recovery timeline, previous-vaccination spacing when known, diagnosis and uncertainty/evidence flags. Preserve parent-report, each reviewer and accepted series separately. Dates/ranges are not reduced to exact histogram bins unless supported.

### 8.8 Duplicates, partials and separate counts

Retain saved partials under the consent scope. Unsent keystrokes are not submitted research answers. Distinguish A1 draft/lock, A2-A4 save, disclosure displayed, continuation, explicit decline and departure without choice. A departure alone supplies no reason. Proposed administrative session closure is 14 days after invitation without deletion; security timeout/resume-token behavior is separately TBD. Stop-contact preferences suppress future research reminders; necessary payment, initiated correction and approved privacy communications remain separate.

Link confirmed same-child submissions using authorized existing contact/link information and neutral clarification, not birthdate alone. Preserve all parents' accounts, sources and conflicts; suspected matches remain unresolved. A confirmed child counts once toward the target and each plot; actual submissions are counted separately. The proposed child-level parent-series convention is the first completed consenting submission by receipt time, with deterministic ID tie-break, frozen before enrollment. It does not discard later accounts or select by favorable timing.

The administrative 100-count requires receipt-time Survey 2 reported phenotype/age eligibility, required answers/narratives/birthdate and an apparently dateable eligible visit document. Later reviewer reclassification does not retrospectively remove that administrative receipt. Survey completion, administrative target, compensation, evidence-supported scientific tiers and permission/privacy-approved public release are separate flags.
