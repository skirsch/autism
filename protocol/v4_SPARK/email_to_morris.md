# Email to Jeffrey Morris — draft

**Subject:** A study designed so that one of us is wrong — would you own the analysis plan?

Jeff,

We've disagreed publicly about whether the regression-timing signals in parent surveys are real or an artifact of who answers and how they remember. I think there's a Day 0–2 clustering after vaccination visits that survives once you control for selection and recall. You think it's what selection and recall look like. Neither of us can settle it with the data that exist, because every dataset that has day-level timing was recruited on the hypothesis, and every dataset that wasn't has month-level resolution.

I'd like to run a study that can settle it, and I'd like you to be the person who makes sure it can. The attached protocol is a pre-registered, self-controlled analysis of parent-observed onset against uploaded immunization records in the SPARK cohort — a frame nobody joined because of vaccines. The confirmatory test is one test, in the stratum where your account predicts nothing: parents who don't attribute their child's autism to vaccines and whose onset date is anchored by photos, video, or messages rather than memory. Non-vaccinating M-CHAT visits and other dated exposures get the identical analysis. Unaided narrative comes first and is locked before anything that could prime it. Section 8.6 spells out in advance what result supports which account, and once we pre-register, neither of us can add to it.

The structure I'm proposing: you own the analysis code and run the primary analysis; an academic PI owns data collection; I fund it as an unrestricted gift to the PI's institution with no access to raw data and no say over the manuscript; we submit as a Registered Report and don't collect a single response until the journal has accepted the design; and everyone signs a commitment to publish whichever way it comes out. My name goes in the conflict-of-interest statement along with my public position on the question, which is where it belongs.

Section 14 is yours. It asks what controls, thresholds, or exclusions you'd need in place to accept a positive result — and I'm asking you to be as demanding as you'd be reviewing it cold. If the answer is that no retrospective design could convince you, I'd want to hear that too, and why, because the companion prospective study in §13 exists to cover whatever this one can't.

If you're willing, a 30-minute call to hear your first objections would be the right next step. If you're not, I'd still value a written note on §14.

Steve
