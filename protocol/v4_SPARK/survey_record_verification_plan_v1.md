# Survey Record Verification Plan

**Version:** 1.0  
**Date:** September 4, 2026

## Purpose

This plan describes how survey responses will be checked against contemporaneous records before the primary timing analysis. Verification is intended to measure recall error and determine whether the reported relationship between developmental onset, well-child visits, and vaccination remains present in record-supported data.

Verification does not establish the proportion of all autism attributable to any cause. It supports the narrower analysis of whether abrupt, lasting developmental onset is temporally associated with vaccination rather than merely with a pediatrician visit.

## Facts to verify

For each selected response, verify these facts separately:

1. **Onset date or onset window:** The first day the parent noticed a clear, lasting developmental plateau, loss of skills, or rapid behavioral change—not the diagnosis date.
2. **Preceding well-child visit:** The date and type of the most recent scheduled well-child visit before onset.
3. **Vaccination at that visit:** Whether any vaccines were administered, and which vaccines, if recorded.
4. **Intervening vaccination:** Whether the child received a vaccine anywhere else after that well-child visit and before onset.
5. **Sequence on the same day:** When onset and the visit occurred on the same date, whether onset was first observed before or after the visit and vaccination.
6. **Pre-existing concerns:** Whether developmental or behavioral concerns were documented before or at the visit.

Each fact must receive its own verification status. Verification of the visit date, for example, must not automatically be treated as verification of the onset date or vaccination status.

## Acceptable evidence

Evidence may include:

- Pediatric, vaccination, pharmacy, hospital, or public-health records.
- Vaccination cards or immunization-registry records.
- Dated emails, text messages, patient-portal messages, calendar entries, diaries, photographs, or videos.
- Dated social-media posts or other contemporaneous communications.
- Other records whose date and relevance can be independently evaluated.

Later recollection without contemporaneous support remains parent-reported and must not be labeled record-verified.

## Selection of records

The verification sample and rules must be fixed before examining verification outcomes.

- Seek verification for all eligible cases in the primary early post-visit window when feasible.
- Seek verification for all confirmed no-vaccination comparison cases when feasible.
- Select a reproducible random sample from later comparison windows.
- Include cases whose reported timing does not support a vaccination association; do not select only dramatic or concordant accounts.
- Report the number invited, responding, supplying records, and successfully verified in each analysis group.

If resources permit, verification of every analyzable response is preferred.

## Review procedure

1. Freeze the survey export, analysis definitions, and verification-sampling list.
2. Assign each selected response a study identifier and remove unnecessary identifying information from the review materials.
3. Whenever practical, have the record reviewer determine dates and events without seeing the respondent's claimed interval or the study hypothesis.
4. Use two independent reviewers for ambiguous records. Resolve disagreements by a third reviewer or a documented consensus procedure.
5. Preserve an audit trail containing the evidence type, extracted facts, reviewer decisions, and reasons for any indeterminate classification.
6. Do not alter the original survey response. Store verified values in separate fields.

## Verification classifications

Classify each relevant fact as:

- **Confirmed:** The record supports the reported fact within the prespecified tolerance.
- **Corrected:** A record supplies a different value that is sufficiently precise for analysis.
- **Contradicted:** The available record conflicts with the reported fact and does not support a defensible corrected value.
- **Indeterminate:** Evidence is missing, incomplete, internally inconsistent, or too imprecise.
- **Not requested or not supplied:** Verification was not attempted or the participant did not provide records.

Absence of a vaccination entry on an incomplete checklist is not confirmation that no vaccination occurred. A case belongs in the confirmed no-vaccination group only when the relevant record adequately establishes that no vaccine was administered at the visit. If vaccination occurred between the visit and onset at another location, the case must not be analyzed as an unexposed no-shot control.

## Date tolerances

Before reviewing records, specify the allowable agreement for each measure. At minimum, report:

- Exact agreement for visit and vaccination dates when clinical records are available.
- Exact-day or bounded-window agreement for onset, according to the precision of the contemporaneous evidence.
- Whether any corrected value changes the case's prespecified analysis window.

Do not convert an imprecise date into an exact date. If the evidence spans more than one analysis bin, classify the bin assignment as indeterminate.

## Analysis and reporting

Report results separately for:

1. All eligible parent-reported responses.
2. Responses for which verification was attempted.
3. Responses with sufficient records for the relevant comparison.
4. Record-confirmed or record-corrected responses.

For vaccination timing, distinguish at least:

- Well-child visit with confirmed vaccination.
- Well-child visit with confirmed no vaccination and no intervening vaccination before onset.
- Vaccination occurring outside the preceding well-child visit.
- Vaccination status indeterminate.

Repeat the prespecified timing analysis using verified or corrected values. Report how often verification confirmed, corrected, contradicted, or could not resolve each key fact, and how corrections changed analysis-bin assignments.

Compare participants who supplied usable records with those who did not on available survey characteristics, including reported interval, onset-date confidence, vaccination status, birth year, and source or referral channel. This assesses whether the verified subset is systematically different from the full sample.

## Interpretation

- A timing pattern limited to confirmed vaccination visits, and absent after confirmed no-vaccination visits, would weigh against a generic pediatrician-visit anchoring explanation.
- Similar clustering after both vaccination and confirmed no-vaccination visits would support a visit-related, recall-related, or other shared explanation rather than an injection-specific timing pattern.
- A materially weaker association after record verification would indicate that recall or classification error contributed to the parent-reported result.
- Small, selectively verified, or imprecise comparison groups must be reported as inconclusive rather than as evidence of no association.

All conclusions must be limited to the population and onset phenotype represented by the study. A temporal association alone does not estimate an attributable fraction and should not be generalized to all autism without appropriate population denominators and a design capable of supporting that inference.
