# Two-Survey Autism Regression Study Overview

**Version:** 2.61 draft
**Date:** October 4, 2026
**Status:** Draft for study-team and IRB development; not approved for recruitment

Supporting operational drafts and the IPAK-format application are indexed in [submission_checklist.md](submission_checklist.md). These implement the agreed design; institutional confirmations, actual platform tests, final signatures and IRB approval remain pending. Stage-specific participant terms are in [consent_and_disclosure.md](consent_and_disclosure.md); the specialized manuals do not establish that controls have been deployed.

## 1. Study in brief

This is a data-collection and documentation study. Its principal scientific product is a redacted public-use dataset, with restricted source evidence available to qualified researchers through an approved controlled-access process. The study team's plots are descriptive aids to understanding the collected data, not formal tests of vaccine effects or causal estimates.

This study will use two sequential parent surveys and a records-validation phase to identify and investigate sudden-onset regressive autism (SORA): an identifiable developmental change after a child had apparently been developing normally. The detailed Survey 2/records cohort will focus on children whose ONSET occurred at 6 through 24 completed months of age. ONSET is the single date when either parent first noticed the qualifying lasting behavior or skill change. Earlier illness, distress, uncertain functioning, and returns to normal remain separately described timeline information; they do not create another onset endpoint. The study will not recruit a separate cohort of children without regression; all authorized possible-case referrals may complete Survey 2, including low-confidence and uncertain reports. The partner keeps its screen answers; age, phenotype, certainty and evidence-tier distinctions are made from study-collected answers rather than transferred partner responses.

The study team will first approach large autism membership organizations to ask whether they are willing to invite their members to complete Survey 1. If that approach does not secure a participating organization, the team will approach autism diagnosis and/or treatment clinics. Recruitment will begin only after a partner agrees and the applicable IRB approvals and arrangements are in place. Survey 1 is a two-question, cause-neutral partner contact-interest screen kept in the partner's own records. It asks about a possible sudden-onset child and explicit interest/permission to share an email with the study team; it does not collect research dates, onset age or records-confidence. Only authorized emails are transferred, with administrative source/batch and permission attestations, not individual screening answers. The partner's regulatory role and privacy/retention terms require confirmation; this arrangement is not automatically outside research oversight. It identifies families reporting that a child progressed normally and then had an identifiable date when a clear change in behavior or skills associated with the child's later autism presentation was first noticed by one or both parents. Its invitation, first-stage purpose statement, consent, and questions will not mention vaccination or disclose a preferred exposure hypothesis. The exact first-stage disclosure and any later staged disclosure require IRB approval.

Every authorized possible-case referral receives a Survey 2 invitation within the approved recruitment window. Low confidence does not exclude participation: the study collects the parent's best estimates, uncertainty and confidence. Records-confidence is collected in Survey 2 after the locked original account and cause-neutral timing answers but before named-event disclosure. It determines only the order of later requests for supporting materials, with the highest-confidence group asked first and lower-confidence groups opened as needed toward 100 objectively complete submissions. Before vaccination or other named candidate events are disclosed in study materials, Survey 2 asks the parent to reconstruct, in their own words, the sequence of events during the 15 days before the first clear developmental change. The narrative must be completed and saved before the participant reaches the additional disclosure and subsequent event-specific questions. A separate cause-neutral, pre-disclosure question will capture the most recent period of uncertain normal functioning before that change, whether it was continuous with the change or separated by a full return to normal and however far back it began. After disclosure, a separate prompted timeline will cover the days immediately before the relevant visit through ONSET, including all normal periods counted in B and non-normal or unassessable periods. Survey 2 also asks about timing, uncertainty, available supporting materials, and willingness to provide records.

The study will then seek supporting records from eligible respondents, prioritizing those who reported the greatest confidence that relevant records are available. The recruitment target is 100 administratively complete documented-visit submissions, counting vaccination and qualifying vaccine-free well-visit submissions together. This is a threshold for ending new records-request batches, not a cap on submissions from people already asked or a confidence gate on Survey 2. Request the most recent actual vaccination on/before parent-first-notice ONSET regardless of how long before it occurred. Separately, where no vaccination occurred within 90 days of ONSET, collect a qualifying vaccine-free regular well-child visit within that period, if any. Respondents with neither qualifying anchor remain retained. Selection for a records request uses prespecified eligibility and availability criteria—not vaccination status, timing, or whether an account supports a hypothesis.

This sequence is intended to provide:

1. A broad, cause-neutral intake with a documented recruitment and response funnel, subject to the reach of the participating recruitment source.
2. An unprompted account of events completed before vaccination or other named candidate events are disclosed or asked about in study materials, subject to IRB approval of the staged disclosure.
3. A records-supported subset in which onset timing and actual vaccination history can be assessed more accurately.
4. Transparent reporting of participation, attrition, record availability, and selection into each study stage.

The study can describe temporal patterns and the quality of parent-reported timing. It cannot, by itself, establish that vaccination or any other event caused autism or regression.

## 2. Primary research questions

The study will address five questions:

1. At what ages do parents first notice the qualifying lasting developmental change among children described as developing normally beforehand?
2. How closely does the parent-first-notice ONSET date agree with contemporaneous records or other dated evidence?
3. Among participants with adequate records, what are the distributions of vaccination-to-ONSET days (A) and total normal days in that window (B), and how might A be compared descriptively with calendar-quarter and nominal CDC-schedule reference lags (C)?
4. Among participants with no vaccination in the 90 days before ONSET but a vaccine-free regularly scheduled well-child visit in that window, what is the visit-to-ONSET lag, and how does its descriptive distribution compare with the vaccination-to-ONSET distribution?
5. What other illnesses, medical encounters, medications, environmental changes, or acute events are reported or documented shortly before onset?

These are data-collection and descriptive questions, not confirmatory hypothesis tests. The collection and review rules will be documented before enrollment; timing analyses chosen or revised after the dataset is examined will be identified as exploratory. Any later inferential analysis by this or another team will require its own explicit methods and assumptions appropriate to the released data.

## 3. Study population and recruitment

### Recruitment partners and sequence

The study team will first ask large autism membership organizations whether they are willing to participate and distribute the approved Survey 1 invitation to their members. If no such organization agrees, the team will approach autism diagnosis and/or treatment clinics. No recruitment source, mailing-list size, or invitation count is assumed before a partner is confirmed. The invitation, Survey 1 purpose statement and consent, and first survey will use neutral language and will not mention vaccination. The IRB will review whether the proposed first-stage description is adequate or whether an alteration of consent is needed.

For each participating source, the number of people actually invited is an invitation denominator, not the study sample size. The study will separately report, to the extent technically available:

- Invitations attempted and successfully delivered.
- Undeliverable messages and opt-outs.
- Survey links opened.
- Partner screen starts/completions and aggregate Yes/No/Unsure counts, where available.
- Authorized email referrals and study invitations; individual partner screen answers are not transferred.
- Study respondents inside, outside, or uncertain with respect to the 6–24-completed-month onset-age rule, assessed in Survey 2.
- Study-collected phenotype and confidence distributions.
- Survey 2 invitations, starts, and completions.
- Survey 2 participants who reach the first vaccination disclosure, continue afterward, decline, or stop without stating a reason; the exact disclosure point and corresponding denominators will be prespecified.
- Parents willing to provide records.
- Records requested, submitted, usable, and unavailable.
- Reasons for noncompletion or exclusion when known.

If the first participating source does not yield 100 objectively complete record submissions, the study may solicit participation from up to three additional autism membership organizations, diagnostic clinics, or treatment clinics. Additional sources will be added sequentially as needed to reach the target. Each must use the IRB-approved recruitment materials and study instruments. Recruitment source, invitation denominator, response funnel, and record yield will be tracked and reported separately; samples will not be silently pooled.

### Child-selection rule

Each family will answer about one child. The following rule will be stated identically in the invitation, consent, surveys, and analysis plan:

- If the family has more than one autistic child, answer about the youngest child who progressed normally and then had an identifiable date, at age 6 through 24 completed months, when a clear change in behavior or skills was first noticed by one or both parents.
- If no child fits that description, answer about the youngest autistic child.

This rule is designed to identify children with a clearly dateable developmental change while preventing a parent from choosing among multiple qualifying children after seeing later study questions. It creates a selected-child sample and does not estimate the prevalence of skill-loss regression or new-behavior onset among all autistic children or in the general population.

The broad intake and main parent-report cohort are exploratory. A parent's report that the child has autism is sufficient at Survey 1; a professional diagnosis or diagnostic record is not an intake eligibility requirement. Diagnostic history and any available supporting documentation may be collected in the smaller Survey 2/records subset and reported by evidence status. Lack of diagnostic documentation will not by itself discard a submitted case, and parent-reported cases will not be described as clinically verified.

### Onset-age rule for detailed follow-up

All authorized possible-case referrals may complete Survey 2; the partner screen does not establish age eligibility or date precision. The detailed evidence-supported cohort remains focused on ONSET at **6 through 24 completed months inclusive**. Survey 2 collects the child's birthdate and parent-first-notice date/bounds; unknown, low-confidence and out-of-range reports are retained with separate flags, not screened out of survey access. This rule uses first notice, not vaccination, diagnosis, earlier illness or full-regression dates. The records-request pool and administrative target use receipt-time parent-reported phenotype and age eligibility established in Survey 2. Later reviewer reclassification does not retroactively change an otherwise objectively complete administrative count; scientific age/phenotype tiers remain separate. Payment remains activity-based. Do not widen the detailed cohort after examining yields or timing results.

## 4. Key definitions

### Previously progressing normally

The child had acquired and consistently demonstrated age-appropriate skills or behaviors before the reported change, without a known earlier pattern of developmental regression. The final instrument will collect concrete examples of the child's prior developmental baseline rather than relying only on the parent's category selection. A newly appearing behavior does not require a simultaneous loss of a previously acquired skill.

### Sudden-onset regressive autism (SORA)

A clear change in behavior or skills associated with the child's later autism presentation, first noticeable on an identifiable date by one or both parents after a period of apparently normal development. Either loss or substantial reduction of a previously acquired skill, or the clear new appearance of a marked behavior without skill loss, can qualify. For example, the first noticeable onset of head banging can qualify as a new-behavior presentation. Eligibility does not require the change to unfold or become fully recognizable within seven days.

The study will code skill loss/reduction and new-behavior onset as separate, nonexclusive flags, then report mutually exclusive loss-only, new-behavior-only, and mixed groups separately as well as together. The broader study label does not imply that every qualifying child experienced loss of an acquired skill; conventional skill-loss regression will not be inferred from a new behavior alone.

For this protocol, “sudden onset” means that the parent can identify when the first clear change became noticeable. It does not mean that every affected behavior changed on one day or that the full developmental change was completed within a fixed number of days.

Examples of relevant domains include language, social engagement, eye contact, play, motor abilities, adaptive skills, and the appearance of marked repetitive behavior or sensory disturbance. The survey will collect the concrete observation rather than asking the parent to make a clinical judgment that a behavior was intrinsically autistic. A temporary change during an illness that fully resolves is not, by itself, a qualifying developmental change.

### ONSET: parent-first-notice date

The single ONSET date is the identifiable date when either parent first noticed the qualifying persistent loss/reduction of a skill or marked new behavior. It is not an inferred biological onset, the date of an earlier illness or uncertain period, the date of diagnosis, a clinician's documentation date, or the date the full developmental change was complete. Preserve the parent's original reported date, any later clarification, each reviewer's assessment, and the accepted value separately.

### Earlier observations and timing uncertainty

Record the last clearly usual observation, earlier illness or uncertain-functioning periods, and any full returns to baseline as timeline information. Whether functioning returned fully is the parent's assessment, with evidence and contradictions recorded separately. Do not replace ONSET with vaccination, symptom onset, or the start of a period when functioning could not be assessed.

Record earliest/latest plausible dates and precision for the parent's first notice, without inventing an exact day. Observation bounds—such as the last confirmed presence of a skill and first confirmed persistent loss, or the last adequate baseline observation without a new behavior and its first definite observation—are separate contextual evidence. They do not create another onset endpoint or establish the unobserved biological start of a change. Unknown bounds remain unknown; a dated after-change item demonstrates that the behavior was present by its date, not necessarily when it was first noticed.

### Objectively complete record submission

A submission with receipt-time parent-reported phenotype and 6–24-month age eligibility established in Survey 2, with all required Survey 2 fields, including the 15-day narrative, the separate pre-disclosure continuity answer, and parent-entered child birthdate, plus documentation of the reported most recent actual vaccination on/before parent-reported ONSET without a maximum lookback, or a qualifying vaccine-free regularly scheduled well-child visit within 90 days. This is the administrative definition for the 100 documented-visit recruitment target, not the definition of a complete compensated response or a guarantee of 100 age-eligible analytic cases. Administrative staff check that the fields and an apparently dateable qualifying visit document are present; the three reviewers do not decide whether a submission counts toward the recruitment target. A later Survey 2 age discrepancy or reviewer assessment changing the accepted ONSET date does not retroactively change this administrative count. Documentary proof of the child's birthdate is not required. The final records manual will separately define scientific usability and eligibility for each analysis.

## 5. Study flow

### Stage 1: Survey 1—partner contact-interest screen

The partner administers a brief screen for its own records: (1) a possible sudden-onset child after apparently normal development, allowing uncertainty; (2) interest in a study invitation and explicit permission to pass an email to the identified study team. No birth year, onset age/date, date confidence, records-confidence or candidate-event information is collected here. The partner keeps individual responses under its own disclosed policy; the study receives only authorized emails plus administrative source/batch and permission attestations, and available aggregate recruitment counts. Permission for contact is not research consent.

### Survey 2 invitations and later records-request phases

Every authorized possible-case referral receives a Survey 2 invitation within the approved recruitment window, without records-confidence or date-confidence selection. Obtain research consent and applicable legal authority before answers. A1 remains the first substantive question; A2 obtains first notice and date confidence, A3 captures uncertain functioning, and neutral A4 collects records-confidence before named-event disclosure. Low confidence, best estimates and unknowns are retained.

Only later requests for supporting materials use four ordered groups:

1. **Definitely available now.**
2. **Probably available; I know how to obtain them.**
3. **Might be available; I would need to investigate.**
4. **Probably unavailable.**

There is no fifth ranked group. Missing/unsure confidence remains explicitly unclassified and may be clarified neutrally; it never blocks Survey 2. Request selection requires consent/contact permission and receipt-time reported phenotype/6–24-month eligibility, not a review result or actual vaccination status. Within groups, use recorded seeded random ordering. Initially ask 100 eligible consenting candidates from Group 1, filling from successive groups if needed. After each records-request batch's 14-day window, use its mature request count `I_m` and administrative documented-visit yield `C_m`. With total `C < 100`, next request batch is `ceil(1.20 * (100-C) / (C_m/I_m))`; if yield is zero/unestimable, ask another 100 or the remainder. Cap at available unrequested candidates and exhaust confidence groups in order.

The seed/method, pool snapshots and attribution rules are fixed before dispatch. Request sizing and new-source decisions use administrative counts only, not reviewer judgments, date precision, vaccine products/status/timing, causal belief or interim histograms. Staff who send requests receive a minimized eligibility/confidence/permission queue without exposure fields. Stop opening new records requests at 100; retain all already requested submissions. Survey 2 invitation access and its enrollment/end rules are separate from this records target; record any approved operational ceiling transparently rather than presenting the records phases as survey selection.

### Stage 2: Survey 2—unprompted reconstruction followed by structured questions

Survey 2 will first ask the parent, without naming vaccination or any other candidate event in the invitation, information and consent materials, or survey questions shown so far, to describe the sequence of events beginning 15 days before the first definite developmental change and ending with that change. The narrative should ask for enough concrete detail to help reviewers assess whether the reported change matches the study definition, including what the child could do before the change, what first changed, who noticed it, and how the change developed afterward. After this original narrative is completed and locked, but still before any named-event disclosure or question, Survey 2 will ask two simple cause-neutral timing questions: (1) when the parent first clearly noticed the persistent developmental change; and (2) whether there had been any earlier period when the parent could not tell whether the child was functioning normally, even if the child later seemed fully normal again. If there were several, use the most recent such period before the clear change. Ask approximately when it began and ended, whether and when the parent judged that the child returned fully to the prior normal baseline, with timing uncertainty preserved. Do not impose a 15-day or 90-day lookback limit on this question, force an exact date from an approximate duration, or name vaccination or a suggested cause.

The survey platform will display one question at a time. The unprompted narrative, cause-neutral timing/date-confidence questions and neutral A4 records-confidence answer must be saved before any named-event disclosure; A1 is locked against later edits. The participant then receives the IRB-approved additional disclosure explaining questions about medical and other events, including vaccination, and possible records requests. The participant may decline; no event-specific questions or records request follows without affirmative agreement. Those who continue provide a separate visit-to-ONSET chronology and structured event questions. Later answers never overwrite the original narrative. Only supporting-material requests, not survey participation, are prioritized by A4 confidence.

Delaying the first vaccination disclosure until after the narrative is a fixed design requirement intended to avoid priming recall. The study will seek an explicit IRB determination on whether this sequence requires an alteration of informed consent and, if so, approval of the alteration and any subsequent disclosure. The study will not implement the sequence without that approval; if it is not approved, the protocol must be reconsidered rather than moving vaccination disclosure before the narrative without a study-team decision. The system will log narrative completion, display of the additional disclosure, affirmative continuation, explicit decline, and departure without a recorded choice as distinct states. Use, retention, and any public release of a pre-disclosure narrative from a participant who does not continue must be addressed expressly in the approved protocol and first-stage consent.

Survey 2 will collect or confirm:

- Whether the first clear change involved loss or substantial reduction of a previously acquired skill, a clearly new behavior, or both.
- Separate first-clear-change-day descriptions of each new behavior and each previously demonstrated skill or behavior that was reduced or absent, including explicit none/unknown answers, prior baseline, observer, and persistence.
- For skill loss or reduction: the specific prior skill, its last confirmed presence, and its first confirmed persistent loss or substantial reduction.
- For a new behavior: a concrete description and examples (such as head banging), the last adequate observation of the prior baseline without it when known, and its first definite observation.
- Parent-observed first definite developmental-change date, with any plausible-date bounds recorded separately.
- Whether any uncertain-functioning period occurred before the first definite developmental change; if so, describe the most recent one, its approximate start and end when known, whether full normal functioning returned in the parent's judgment, with any timing uncertainty. Record unknown dates or duration rather than infer them from vaccination.
- The onset uncertainty interval.
- Persistence of the change.
- Parent-reported professional autism diagnosis status and, if known, the diagnosing clinician type and approximate diagnosis date; diagnostic documentation may be submitted if available but is not required for participation.
- The unprompted 15-day event narrative.
- A separate post-disclosure chronological account of the days immediately before the most recent pre-change vaccination or applicable vaccine-free visit, the visit day, all normal periods counted in B, and non-normal/unassessable periods until ONSET, with approximate dates or order when recalled and unknown intervals preserved.
- Records or dated materials believed to be available.
- Permission to be contacted for the records phase.
- The date or age of the most recent regularly scheduled well-child visit within 90 days before the first definite developmental change, if any.
- Whether any vaccine was actually administered at that visit: Yes, No, or Unknown.
- Whether any vaccination occurred after that well-child visit but before the first definite developmental change: Yes, No, or Unknown.
- For a reported vaccination visit before ONSET: whether any vaccine was given after that visit but before ONSET. If Yes, ask for the later, most recent visit's date/products and request its record. Preserve the correction history.
- For the most recent pre-onset vaccination visit: whether the parent understood the child to be following the routine recommended vaccination schedule at that time, a delayed or alternative plan, or was unsure. This is a parent-reported description, not a determination of adherence to a particular year's schedule.
- For that vaccination visit: whether illness or anything unusual was present in the days beforehand, whether the child was at their usual behavior-and-skill baseline immediately before administration, and the last definitely usual observation if not.
- For every child with a reported pre-onset vaccination, regardless of schedule category: the date of the immediately preceding vaccination visit, if known; otherwise an approximate interval since that visit, "no earlier vaccination," or "don't know." Count visits on distinct dates, not the number of vaccine products given at one visit. Calculate the spacing from two dates when available. The preceding visit need not be documented to answer this survey question; mark its date or interval as parent-reported unless independently supported.
- Whether the child has ever received any vaccination, anywhere and to the parent's knowledge: Yes, No, or Unsure. A No answer defines **parent-reported never vaccinated** without mandatory documentary proof; it is separate from whether any vaccination occurred within 90 days before the first definite developmental change.
- Whether any regularly scheduled well-child visit occurred within 90 days before the first definite developmental change: Yes, No, or Unsure. If neither a vaccination nor a well-child visit occurred in that period, record both absences rather than forcing a visit date.
- After named-event disclosure, ask about post-vaccination acute symptoms, their start/end, and whether they persisted through ONSET, allowing approximate/unknown answers. Separately ask B: the total number of complete normal days between the selected vaccination and ONSET, adding all disconnected normal periods. Normal means the directly observed pre-vaccination behavior-and-skill baseline, not merely symptom resolution. Count vaccination day only if normal throughout that day; exclude ONSET day and all pre-vaccination days. For known values, 0 ≤ B ≤ A; if A = 0, B = 0. Preserve exact, approximate, lower-bound, unknown, and baseline-unestablished answers, and identify non-normal versus unassessable days. A/B are not applicable without an eligible vaccination anchor. Check B against all normal periods in the prompted narrative, logging discrepancies rather than overwriting answers.

The original unprompted narrative will be saved immediately and preserved as submitted. It may be used, together with the structured before-and-after skill questions, to assess whether the reported event satisfies the onset definition. Later prompts, clarifications, or record review will be stored separately and will not overwrite the original account.

If a participant stops after submitting the narrative but before completing Survey 2, the proposed policy is to retain the narrative and all previously submitted answers as a partial response, including when the participant declines at the additional disclosure. No submitted survey answer will be discarded merely because the participant stops at disclosure; the partial-response status and point of departure will be retained for analysis. The consent presented before any study questions must disclose this proposed partial-response retention, the planned redacted public release described below, and the study's no-revocation policy. This treatment of pre-disclosure narratives requires specific IRB approval. The analysis plan will specify which descriptive or eligibility assessments may use partial responses and which analyses require a completed Survey 2 or usable records.

### Stage 3: records request and validation

All authorized possible-case referrals may complete Survey 2. Later supporting-material request waves depend on the original neutral Survey 2 records-confidence response within the consented, reported-age/phenotype-eligible pool. Do not rank by onset-date precision, vaccination status, proximity, product, causal belief or expected hypothesis support. Because later Survey 2 answers may contain exposure information, request-selection staff use a minimized queue excluding those fields; unlike the old workflow, exposure information is no longer necessarily unknown to the study.

The study will seek at least 100 objectively complete record submissions from respondents screened as eligible for the 6–24-completed-month onset-age cohort. This count includes submissions with documentation of the most recent vaccination before the parent-reported first definite developmental change and submissions with documentation of a vaccine-free regularly scheduled well-child visit before that change. Administrative completeness is determined by the presence of the required survey fields and documents, before the three reviewers assess the ONSET date or decide which records qualify for each analysis. If the first participating source does not yield 100 complete submissions, recruitment may continue sequentially through as many as three additional approved sources. Every submission received from an open invitation batch will be retained and reviewed, even if the total exceeds 100. The study will report the number of submitted records that are scientifically usable after review, including later age-boundary discrepancies; reviewer decisions will not change the recruitment threshold or trigger additional invitations.

Parents who make a good-faith submission of the requested materials by the stated deadline will receive a $25 Amazon gift card under IRB-approved terms. The $25 payment will be issued even if the submitted material is incomplete, cannot be used in the final analysis, establishes a vaccine-free visit, contradicts the parent's recollection, or does not support any study hypothesis.

### Adaptive compensation if record yield is inadequate

The IRB submission will request advance approval for the following staged compensation schedule for a complete response:

1. Begin at $25.
2. At each two-week operational checkpoint, determine whether 100 objectively complete record submissions have been received.
3. If the target has not been reached, increase compensation by $25 for a complete response: first to $50, then to $75, and finally to the maximum of $100.
4. Stop increasing compensation once 100 objectively complete record submissions have been received or the $100 cap has been reached.

The first two-week checkpoint will run from the date the first Survey 2 invitation batch is released. Each later checkpoint will occur two weeks after the preceding increase. A response qualifies for the full amount then offered when the participant has submitted all required Survey 2 fields, including the 15-day narrative and birthdate, plus at least one requested evidence item: documentation of the most recent vaccination on/before ONSET (any lag) or qualifying well-child visit within 90 days, or an apparently dateable contemporaneous item with onset-related content. A no-visit family that submits onset evidence therefore qualifies for the same full amount, including any later retroactive top-up. Administrative staff verify receipt, apparent dateability, and presence of onset-related content, not whether the evidence proves the onset date; that scientific judgment belongs to the three reviewers. A good-faith participant without a qualifying evidence item still receives the guaranteed $25. Full-payment eligibility does not depend on reviewer judgments, SORA eligibility, evidentiary strength, vaccination status, inclusion in an analysis, or whether the submission supports a hypothesis.

The 100 documented-visit submission count governs compensation-increase checkpoints and the records-request stopping rule, but it is separate from individual eligibility for the full payment. No-visit responses with dated onset evidence receive the full payment without being counted as documented-visit submissions. Reaching 100 stops compensation increases and further records-request batches, not confidence-free Survey 2 access within its approved recruitment window; all submitted material still undergoes the prespecified scientific review, and the final number eligible for each analysis will be reported.

The decision to activate a higher amount may use only the count of objectively complete submissions received by the checkpoint. It may not use vaccination status, exposure timing, reviewer judgments, scientific usability, interim histograms, or whether received records support a hypothesis.

When compensation is increased, every earlier participant who completed the same required activities will be offered a retroactive top-up sufficient to bring that participant's total compensation to the new amount. Thus, participants completing equivalent work will ultimately receive equal compensation regardless of enrollment date. The consent and recruitment materials will state the payment schedule, objective completion requirements, timing and method of payment, retroactive top-up rule, and that payment is compensation for time and effort rather than a study benefit.

No payment may depend on a particular answer, vaccination history, reviewer judgment, eligibility for the primary analysis, or agreement with the study hypothesis. The evidence alternatives reflect which records exist, not a preferred answer. A participant who makes a good-faith submission but cannot produce a qualifying evidence item will retain the guaranteed $25 payment.

The records request will be deliberately narrow. It is expected to include:

- The completed unprompted narrative covering the 15 days before ONSET through ONSET, plus the separate pre-disclosure uncertain-functioning answer.
- The child's birthdate as entered by the parent; separate documentary proof is not required.
- If the parent reports any vaccination on/before ONSET: request the actual date/products record for the most recent such vaccination, with no maximum lookback. A visit 200 days earlier is eligible. If a later vaccination on/before ONSET is reported, request that later visit's record instead; retain earlier observations separately.
- If the parent reports no vaccination within 90 days before the first definite developmental change: documentation of the most recent regularly scheduled well-child visit without vaccination in that same window, if any, including the visit date and, when available, the visit note or immunization record supporting that no vaccine was administered.
- Any available contemporaneous materials that can corroborate or bound the reported onset, such as a pediatrician's note, text or email, existing lawfully recorded voicemail or call, contemporaneous call note, diary entry, dated video or photograph, daycare record, or calendar entry. These will be actively requested. They are not required for the 100 documented-visit administrative count, but an apparently dateable item with relevant onset content can satisfy the full-payment evidence requirement when no visit document exists. A call log showing only that a call occurred, without contemporaneous evidence of what was said, does not by itself corroborate onset. Absence of corroboration will be reported and may prevent inclusion in the primary timing analysis.
- Any available dated material documenting the start, duration, or resolution of a continuous period in which the child was not clearly at the prior baseline before the definite developmental change. This supplies timeline context, not a second onset date, and is not required for the 100 documented-visit administrative count.

If no vaccination on/before ONSET is identified and no qualifying vaccine-free regular well visit within 90 days exists, do not manufacture a visit anchor. Retain the survey and request existing dated onset-related evidence, which can satisfy the full-payment evidence requirement without meeting the 100 documented-visit target. Distinguish never-vaccinated, previously vaccinated with unknown date, and unknown lifetime history. An earlier vaccination more than 90 days before ONSET remains eligible for the vaccination anchor and target. Parent-reported never-vaccinated status remains separately labeled; optional registry/clinical history corroboration is not required for participation or payment.

The study will not require a complete vaccination history. Evidence must reflect actual administration, not a scheduled appointment or routine age recommendation. Parent confirmation of no later vaccination is recorded separately from documentary proof of the selected visit, with no maximum lookback for that vaccination. If the parent is unsure, retain the report with unconfirmed-most-recent status. If a later vaccination on/before ONSET is reported, request its date/products record. Only the independent well-visit comparator retains a 90-day lookback; uncertainty or missing records must not be converted into a claim of no vaccination.

The reviewers—not the parent—will independently extract and assess the key dates and their supporting evidence in one review of all available material. Study staff will then apply prespecified consistency and dataset-construction rules. Parent-reported onset, each reviewer's evidence-informed estimate, and the values accepted by at least two reviewers will remain separate variables.

### One-pass, three-reviewer assessment

The three designated reviewers—Steve Kirsch, Karl Jablonowski, and Brian Hooker—will each independently evaluate every candidate record once, using the same prespecified rubric and all available material: the original unprompted narrative, later Survey 2 answers, uploaded videos, photographs, messages, clinical records, vaccination records, and other evidence. Each reviewer will decide whether the case meets the study's SORA definition (Yes, No, or Uncertain) and complete the date-and-evidence form below. Vaccination information will not be masked. “Independently” means that each reviewer completes, timestamps, and locks an assessment without seeing or discussing either other reviewer's assessment; it does not mean that the reviewers are independent of the study team. If no evidence beyond the parent's narrative supports onset, that fact will be flagged and the case will not be represented as objectively corroborated onset.

For each case, every reviewer will independently complete the same date-and-evidence form:

1. Child's birthdate as entered by the parent, with any discrepancy noted. This is a parent-reported date, not a document-verified date unless supporting evidence happens to be available; separate birthdate proof is not required.
2. The actual date of the most recent documented vaccination on or before ONSET, with no maximum lookback, every product given on that date, and evidence that it is the most recent eligible visit; or **none established / unknown**. Identify actual administration, not a dose inferred from the recommended schedule or a planned visit.
3. Only if no vaccination occurred in that 90-day period before the first definite developmental change, the date of the most recent regularly scheduled well-child visit without shots **within the same 90-day period**, and the evidence for its date and vaccine-free status; or **no qualifying visit / unknown**. A vaccine-free visit alone does not establish absence of vaccination at another visit; the reviewer will separately code whether the 90-day vaccination history is confirmed complete, parent-reported only, or unknown. A no-visit case remains reviewable for phenotype and timing.
4. The single **ONSET date**: when either parent first noticed the qualifying persistent loss/reduction or marked new behavior, with original parent report, clarifications, and supporting/conflicting evidence preserved separately. Do not substitute earlier illness or uncertain functioning. Record earlier periods and full returns as timeline context, including the total normal-day count B when applicable. Separately record the **earliest documented post-change date**, the first date when submitted contemporaneous evidence documents the changed behavior; this is a documentation field, not another onset date.
5. Earliest/latest plausible dates for the parent's first notice, with width in days and precision; or **unknown** if a bound cannot be established. Exact dates have identical bounds and width zero. Keep observation bounds for skill/behavior change separately from uncertainty about first notice. Neither a symptom date nor an after-change item's date automatically supplies ONSET.

Each date or status will have an evidence citation and source classification (contemporaneous document, later parent report, or inference), plus a conflict/insufficiency flag. The selected vaccination has no maximum lookback; flag uncertain ordering or most-recent selection. The 90-day boundary applies only to the independent well-visit comparator and nominal-age reference; flag uncertainty crossing those boundaries. Corroboration and precision are scientific evidence tiers, separate from administrative completeness and the 100-record threshold.

Onset evidence qualifies by its contemporaneous content and credible date, not by medium. A pediatrician's record, text message, voicemail or recorded call, contemporaneous call note, diary entry, or similar item may establish that a specific change was first noticed on a stated date or bound the interval in which it became noticeable. A single credibly dated after-change video showing the relevant behavior also qualifies as objective onset-related evidence; a before-video or other pre-change item is not required to accept the video as evidence. Its date is the earliest **documented post-change observation** if no earlier qualifying item exists. It proves that the behavior was present by that date, not necessarily that it first began on that date. If the video itself or another contemporaneous item records that the change first appeared that day, the reviewer may additionally assess that as support for a same-day onset date. Reviewers will record both when an item was created and the date of onset it describes; a later clinical note repeating an old parental recollection is not itself contemporaneous corroboration of that earlier date. A bare appointment entry, call log, or undated image establishes at most that an encounter or file exists, not when the developmental change began. The manual will prespecify how reviewers evaluate original-versus-copy provenance, timestamps, alterations, conflicting accounts, and before/after evidence. No single format is automatically accepted or rejected; reviewers must explain how the item's actual content supports the selected onset date or bounds.

Evidence-supported vaccination/visit timing displays require a reviewer-agreed parent-first-notice ONSET date at age 6 through 24 completed months, calculated from the parent-entered birthdate, plus dateable evidence supporting that notice and a documented eligible visit. A vaccine-free well-visit group also requires sufficiently established 90-day vaccination history. The records manual will define corroboration and precision before review begins. Cases outside the age range, with unresolved dates/history, or supported only by retrospective narrative remain retained and reported in appropriately labeled descriptive subsets, not represented as objectively corroborated timing cases. An after-only item may support a documentation date without verifying first notice.

Each reviewer will record the evidence supporting and contradicting the judgment. All three assessments will be locked before any reviewer sees the others' judgments. Once locked, a judgment may not be revised to create agreement or in response to an interim timing-analysis result.

**Majority-agreement rule:** After all three assessments are locked, a SORA eligibility judgment, the ONSET date, any other date-form value or status, the separately recorded first documented post-change date, or each numerical developmental-change bound is accepted for subsequent analysis only when at least two reviewers independently agree on that exact judgment or number. Each bound is assessed separately, and its interval width is calculated from the agreed bounds rather than voted on independently. Agreed lower and upper bounds may form an analysis interval only when they are internally consistent; otherwise the interval remains unresolved. The study will not average disparate dates, invent a compromise number, or change a reviewer's locked answer to manufacture agreement. All three original assessments remain in the audit trail. A case without the agreed values required for a particular analysis is retained and reported but excluded from that analysis.

**Analysis dataset construction:** Preserve parent-reported ONSET, each locked reviewer assessment, accepted dates/status/bounds and the separate earliest documented post-change date. Do not fill unresolved fields from another series. Select the most recent actual vaccination on/before ONSET without a maximum lookback, then derive A and B from that same vaccination-to-ONSET period. Independently select a qualifying vaccine-free regular well visit within 90 days when absence of vaccinations in that period is sufficiently established. A child with an older vaccination may have both A/B and a well-visit lag; count the child once in each applicable plot. Well-only/no-anchor cases have A/B not applicable. C uses its specified reference rules. Retain unresolved inputs without invented anchors; label vaccination-to-documentation timing separately. Review is exposure-visible.

Because reviewers see vaccination information in their single assessment, the onset judgments are not exposure-blinded. Independent voting can measure disagreement but cannot eliminate a shared influence of exposure information on onset dating. The study will report the evidence supporting each accepted value and the rates of agreement and unresolved judgments.

The study will report:

- Agreement among the three reviewers on SORA eligibility.
- Agreement across reviewers on the single ONSET date and its bounds.
- The number and proportion of cases with exact three-reviewer agreement.
- The number and proportion with exact two-of-three agreement on each required judgment or value, and the numbers left unresolved with reasons for disagreement.
- The number and proportion of original narratives that spontaneously mention vaccination, and the evidence tier supporting each accepted onset value.

Exact agreement among all three reviewers is the preferred outcome, but two matching independent assessments are sufficient for a value to survive into analysis. Disagreement will be preserved rather than forced into agreement. The validation manual will define the evidence-access workflow, exact-value and interval-bound formats, agreement metrics, consistency checks, evidence tiers, and handling of unresolved fields before review begins. Accepted values will be selected without the reviewers seeing one another's judgments, but with vaccination information available to each reviewer.

## 6. Protection against hypothesis-driven selection

The central design safeguard is the separation of recruitment and records selection from vaccination ascertainment.

Before vaccination information can influence any invitation or record-request decision, the study will freeze and retain an auditable record of:

1. Authorized referral/contact permission and study-collected eligibility.
2. Record-availability assessment.
3. Survey 2 invitation disposition and later records-confidence request group/reason separately.
4. The date each records-request confidence group was opened, separately from Survey 2 invitations.
5. Whether Survey 2 and records invitations were sent.

The study team will not use vaccination status or timing to choose whom to invite, whom to remind, which submissions count toward the 100-record administrative target, or which good-faith submissions to compensate. The later scientific phenotype and onset judgments will be made by the three reviewers with all available evidence, including vaccination information. The study will not describe those judgments as exposure-blinded.

This design reduces selection based on the exposure of interest. It does not eliminate nonresponse, inaccurate recall, incomplete records, differential willingness to participate, or selection associated with membership-organization or clinic recruitment. Those limitations will be measured and reported by recruitment source where possible.

## 8. Data summaries and reusable analysis fields

The first paper reports the recruitment funnel, response/attrition, missingness, evidence availability, reviewer agreement, and technical validation. It is a data-resource paper, not a formal vaccine-effect test. Approved public-use data are released regardless of timing results, subject to consent, IRB conditions, and disclosure-risk review.

The displays and simulations below are possible uses for a separate later analysis paper. Their bins/models are not fixed by this data-collection protocol; choices made after viewing exposure-linked results must be disclosed as exploratory. Collection, coding, adjudication, and release rules must be reproducible before enrollment.

### Core measures and histogram summary

There is one **ONSET date**: when either parent first noticed the qualifying lasting behavior/skill change. Reviewers assess that reported first notice and its support, not an earlier inferred biological onset. Earlier illness or uncertain functioning stays in the timeline.

| Measure | Definition | Core histogram |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Age at ONSET | ONSET minus parent-entered birthdate; age in days and completed months. | One age-at-ONSET histogram. |
| A: vaccination-to-ONSET days | ONSET minus the selected most recent actual vaccination date on or before ONSET, with no maximum lookback. | One A histogram, with optional age/product breakdowns. |
| B: normal-day count (normal interval) | Total complete normal days between that vaccination and ONSET, including disconnected periods. Normal means observed pre-vaccination behavior/skills, not merely symptom resolution. Count dates from vaccination day (only if normal throughout) through the day before ONSET. No pre-vaccination or ONSET-day count. Known values satisfy 0 ≤ B ≤ A; A = 0 implies B = 0. Preserve precision, lower bounds, unknown and baseline-unestablished status. Without a vaccination anchor, A/B are not applicable, not zero. | One B histogram with its own eligible denominator; lower bounds are not exact-count bins. |
| C: reference-to-ONSET days | ONSET minus the most recent eligible reference date on or before ONSET: calendar-quarter start or a fixed nominal vaccination-age grid shared by all children. | One C histogram for each reference system. |

For calendar-quarter C, choose the most recent January 1, April 1, July 1, or October 1 on or before ONSET. For nominal-age C, construct grid dates at **2, 4, 6, 12, 15 and 18 calendar months** after each child's birthdate, then choose its most recent grid date on or before ONSET within 90 days. Do not customize by birth year, add other age points, or invent a reference when none qualifies. Record grid version and calendar arithmetic, using the original birthdate day or the target month's last day if that day does not exist. The detailed cohort remains **6–24 completed months at ONSET**; the 2/4-month reference points do not admit younger children. This is a consistent age benchmark inspired by common vaccination ages, not a literal CDC-mandated schedule, actual dose history, or proven biological clock. Historical product mappings are not required to construct it, and uniform anchors do not guarantee a particular no-effect distribution.

Comparisons show the same-child eligible subset where possible, denominators, precision, missing inputs, and no-anchor cases. Each child appears once in each applicable plot. Multiple products at one visit share the same last-visit anchor; vaccine-product plots overlap and do not isolate individual product effects.

### Descriptive cohort and timing summaries

- Invitation, consent, screening, follow-up, disclosure continuation/decline/drop-off, and record-request/receipt counts. Do not infer why a parent stopped.
- Phenotype, diagnosis evidence, onset-age/confidence, record availability, and evidence tiers, distinguishing parent-report from corroborated information.
- A histogram with day-0–2 and day-0–5 descriptive counts, optionally days 3–5 separately. The windows overlap and are not independent confirmations.
- B histogram and normal/non-normal/unassessable chronology. Do not calculate B simply as A minus symptom duration; repeated normal periods and unknown days must remain visible.
- Weekday histogram from exact accepted ONSET dates, with vaccination weekday for context; uncertain/missing dates reported separately.
- A separately labeled earliest-documented-post-change display if useful, never described as another onset estimate.
- Sensitivity displays based on date precision, plausible first-notice bounds, evidence tiers, and missingness, without silently substituting dates.

Age plots may mark 12, 15, and 18 months and show age at the actual last vaccination for the same-child subset. A broad smooth age curve or constant daily onset hazard is an illustrative no-effect assumption, not a guaranteed observed shape. Recall rounding, recognition at visits, records selection, and age boundaries can affect these plots. The parent-reported age distribution is not independently established vaccine-free natural history.

Optional A subgroups include age at the last vaccination visit below versus at/above 18 months, nominal visit-age labels such as 12/15/18 months when reproducibly mapped, and vaccine products given on that last visit. Exact boundaries, eligibility, bins, and denominators belong in the later display methods. A hypothesized age peak alone does not guarantee rising or falling vaccination-lag histograms.

### Normal days and symptoms

The post-disclosure narrative must describe all normal periods included in B, what behavior/skills were observed, approximate dates or duration, and observers, alongside non-normal/unassessable/unremembered periods. Vaccination itself is not an observed abnormality. Preserve direct B and timeline discrepancies without clamping or rewriting raw answers.

B = 0 means no complete normal day was counted in the window; it does not alone establish no partial return or continuous abnormal functioning. Distinguish no full return, partial-day returns, unassessable functioning, and inadequate recall. Unknown is not zero. Ending fever, crying, or malaise is not automatically return to the pre-vaccination behavior/skill baseline. Symptom-end timing remains a separate optional descriptive field.

No-vaccine-effect alone does not determine B's shape. A, interruptions of normal functioning, symptom durations, age, observation/recall, and selection must be specified in any proposed comparison model. A continuity narrative or low B is descriptive, not itself proof of causation.

### Optional additional reference and simulation displays

Beyond the two core C anchors, later analyses may use birthdate-based 30/60/90-day grids, nominal CDC visits shifted 7/14 days earlier, child three-month age milestones, or randomized pseudo-visit schedules. Choose the most recent eligible anchor on or before the same ONSET, state the origin and applicable lookback, and report missing/no-anchor cases. Different grid lengths have different lag supports, so raw bin heights are not automatically comparable; any phase normalization must be labeled.

Under constant daily onset hazard, exact dates, phase-independent selection, and complete grid cycles, fixed-grid phases are uniform in expectation. A broad smooth age curve may leave the 30-day phase approximately uniform. Incomplete boundary cycles, changing age rates, heaping/recognition, and selection can produce departures without a grid-day causal effect. Nominal schedules need not match actual-dose lag distributions under no vaccine effect because administration variation, missed/extra visits, and the nominal reference's 90-day selection rule affect shape and eligibility; actual-vaccination A has no maximum lookback.

Any randomized pseudo-schedule is generated independently of ONSET, grouping products into visits under stated historical schedules and timing scenarios. Select the last eligible pseudo-visit before ONSET; do not redraw to force a match. Report replicate-specific no-anchor counts, denominators, assumptions, seeds, and code. Constant-hazard or broad age-peak scenarios near 18 months are hypothetical benchmarks, not an empirical unvaccinated onset rate or a causal/randomization test.

### Vaccine-free well-visit and no-anchor groups

Retain children with no vaccination in the 90-day window and a regular vaccine-free well visit in that window. A corroborated visit-to-ONSET plot requires a documented visit, no shots at that visit, sufficiently established absence of vaccinations elsewhere in the window, and an accepted supported ONSET date. Parent-reported and corroborated groups remain separate; one vaccine-free visit record does not establish a vaccine-free 90-day history. Report sparse groups descriptively, not as evidence of equivalence. A/B are not defined using a well-visit anchor.

Children with no eligible vaccination or well visit remain in age, phenotype, narrative, evidence and reference-date summaries for which inputs suffice. Distinguish parent-reported lifetime never-vaccinated, vaccination with unknown date/most-recent status, and unknown history. An identified vaccination more than 90 days earlier is eligible for A/B, not a no-anchor case. Do not invent a visit or call missing corroboration confirmation. No-anchor cases are not an unvaccinated population incidence sample.

The narrow records request does not provide a complete exposure history or full person-time for a self-controlled case series. Such analyses require additional data and a separate design.

### Provenance of later analytic choices

Any later paper states when displays, subgroups, benchmarks, and models were selected relative to access to exposure-linked data. Unchanged ideas recorded before collection may be labeled planned descriptive displays; new or revised post-inspection choices are exploratory. Neither label makes the work confirmatory or validates a reference histogram as a no-effect control.

## 13. Interpretation limits

Recruitment through an autism membership organization or clinic is not a probability sample of all families with autistic children. The study can calculate response, eligibility, follow-up, and record-retrieval rates within each documented invitation funnel. It cannot use an organization's membership list or a clinic's patient pool to estimate autism incidence, population prevalence of identifiable-onset developmental change, or population vaccine risk without additional population-based sampling information.

A temporal concentration of parent-first-notice ONSET dates after vaccination is descriptive and does not by itself establish causation. Interpretation depends on recruitment/records selection, date accuracy and recognition, incomplete exposure histories, age/schedule effects, co-occurring illness and medical care, event-dependent vaccination, and comparator validity. Low B or a reported continuous sequence adds observations but does not resolve these alternatives. Because reviewers see vaccination information, two- or three-reviewer agreement does not eliminate a shared influence on dating first notice. Causal conclusions require appropriate additional evidence and designs.

There is no maximum lookback for the most recent actual vaccination on/before ONSET or its evidence request. A vaccination 200 days earlier remains an eligible anchor and contributes A=200 when exact inputs support it; B describes the same full period. Longer lookbacks may increase recall gaps, unavailable records and uncertainty. Approximate periods or unknown days are retained, not forced into exact counts or B=0. Document most-recent confirmation separately from actual administration; a single visit record does not establish a complete history. Never-vaccinated and unresolved-date/history cases remain retained without invented A/B. Only the independent vaccine-free well-visit comparator and nominal-age reference retain their 90-day rules. Reference histograms have different supports and are not automatically valid no-effect comparators.
