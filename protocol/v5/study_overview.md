# Two-Survey Autism Regression Study Overview

**Version:** 2.34 draft
**Date:** October 1, 2026
**Status:** Draft for study-team and IRB development; not approved for recruitment

## 1. Study in brief

This study will use two sequential parent surveys and a records-validation phase to identify and investigate sudden-onset regressive autism (SORA): an identifiable developmental change after a child had apparently been developing normally. The detailed Survey 2/records cohort will focus on children whose first definite developmental change occurred at 6 through 24 completed months of age. The records phase will distinguish the first break from the prior normal baseline from the first definite observation of the lasting developmental change. These dates can coincide or be separated by a continuous period during which illness, lethargy, distress, or another condition makes the child's usual skills difficult to assess. The study will not recruit a separate cohort of children without regression; families whose Survey 1 answers do not meet the regression and age eligibility rules remain in the intake funnel counts but are not invited to the records phase.

The study team will first approach large autism membership organizations to ask whether they are willing to invite their members to complete Survey 1. If that approach does not secure a participating organization, the team will approach autism diagnosis and/or treatment clinics. Recruitment will begin only after a partner agrees and the applicable IRB approvals and arrangements are in place. Survey 1 is a short, cause-neutral intake survey. It identifies families reporting that a child progressed normally and then had an identifiable date when a clear change in behavior or skills associated with the child's later autism presentation was first noticed by one or both parents. Its invitation, first-stage purpose statement, consent, and questions will not mention vaccination or disclose a preferred exposure hypothesis. The exact first-stage disclosure and any later staged disclosure require IRB approval.

Eligible parents who agree to follow-up will be invited to Survey 2 in prespecified phases based on their Survey 1 confidence that relevant records are available. The highest-confidence group will be invited first. If that phase produces fewer than 100 objectively complete record submissions, the next confidence group will be invited, continuing as needed until the target is reached. Before vaccination or other named candidate events are disclosed in study materials, Survey 2 asks the parent to reconstruct, in their own words, the sequence of events during the 15 days before the first clear developmental change. The narrative must be completed and saved before the participant reaches the additional disclosure and subsequent event-specific questions. A separate cause-neutral, pre-disclosure question will capture the most recent period of uncertain normal functioning before that change, whether it was continuous with the change or separated by a full return to normal and however far back it began. After disclosure, a separate prompted timeline will cover the days immediately before the relevant visit and notable events through the clear change. Survey 2 also asks about timing, uncertainty, available supporting materials, and willingness to provide records.

The study will then seek supporting records from eligible respondents, prioritizing those who reported the greatest confidence that relevant records are available. The minimum recruitment target is 100 objectively complete record submissions, counting vaccination and vaccine-free well-visit submissions together. This is a threshold for ending new invitation batches, not a cap on submissions from people already invited. The visit-record request uses the 90 days before the first definite developmental change, so it can capture vaccination during an earlier continuous baseline-break episode. Respondents with no vaccination and no well-child visit in that window will remain in the study even though they have no qualifying visit anchor. Selection for a records request will be based on prespecified eligibility and record-availability criteria—not on vaccination status, vaccine timing, or whether a parent's account supports any study hypothesis.

This sequence is intended to provide:

1. A broad, cause-neutral intake with a documented recruitment and response funnel, subject to the reach of the participating recruitment source.
2. An unprompted account of events completed before vaccination or other named candidate events are disclosed or asked about in study materials, subject to IRB approval of the staged disclosure.
3. A records-supported subset in which onset timing and actual vaccination history can be assessed more accurately.
4. Transparent reporting of participation, attrition, record availability, and selection into each study stage.

The study can evaluate temporal associations and the quality of parent-reported timing. It cannot, by itself, establish that vaccination or any other event caused autism or regression.

## 2. Primary research questions

The study will address five questions:

1. At what ages do parents report the first clear developmental change among children who were described as developing normally beforehand, and when did any immediately preceding continuous break from the normal baseline begin?
2. How closely do parent-reported baseline-break and definite-developmental-change dates agree with contemporaneous records or other dated evidence?
3. Among participants with adequate records, how do the lags from the documented vaccination visit to (a) the baseline-break date and (b) the first definite developmental-change date compare, including cases in which vaccination followed the baseline break? Does either show a short-lag excess beyond its separately specified comparison model?
4. Among participants with no vaccination in the 90 days before the first definite developmental change but a vaccine-free regularly scheduled well-child visit in that window, what are the separate lags from that visit to the baseline break and to the first definite change, and how do their distributions compare with the corresponding post-vaccination plots?
5. What other illnesses, medical encounters, medications, environmental changes, or acute events are reported or documented shortly before onset?

The primary inferential target is temporal association, not causation. Analyses of particular vaccines, products, age groups, or newly observed patterns will be identified in advance as confirmatory or reported transparently as exploratory.

## 3. Study population and recruitment

### Recruitment partners and sequence

The study team will first ask large autism membership organizations whether they are willing to participate and distribute the approved Survey 1 invitation to their members. If no such organization agrees, the team will approach autism diagnosis and/or treatment clinics. No recruitment source, mailing-list size, or invitation count is assumed before a partner is confirmed. The invitation, Survey 1 purpose statement and consent, and first survey will use neutral language and will not mention vaccination. The IRB will review whether the proposed first-stage description is adequate or whether an alteration of consent is needed.

For each participating source, the number of people actually invited is an invitation denominator, not the study sample size. The study will separately report, to the extent technically available:

- Invitations attempted and successfully delivered.
- Undeliverable messages and opt-outs.
- Survey links opened.
- Survey 1 starts, consents, and completions.
- Respondents meeting each developmental-pattern category.
- Respondents inside, outside, or uncertain with respect to the 6–24-completed-month onset-age rule.
- Respondents eligible for Survey 2.
- Survey 2 invitations, starts, and completions.
- Survey 2 participants who reach the first vaccination disclosure, continue afterward, decline, or stop without stating a reason; the exact disclosure point and corresponding denominators will be prespecified.
- Parents willing to provide records.
- Records requested, submitted, usable, and unavailable.
- Reasons for noncompletion or exclusion when known.

If the first participating source does not yield 100 objectively complete record submissions, the study may solicit participation from up to three additional autism membership organizations, diagnostic clinics, or treatment clinics. Additional sources will be added sequentially as needed to reach the target. Each must use the IRB-approved recruitment materials and study instruments. Recruitment source, invitation denominator, response funnel, and record yield will be tracked and reported separately; samples will not be silently pooled.

### Child-selection rule

Each family will answer about one child. The following rule will be stated identically in the invitation, consent, surveys, and analysis plan:

- If the family has more than one autistic child, answer about the youngest child who progressed normally and then had an identifiable date, at age 6 through 24 completed months, when a clear change in behavior or skills was first noticed by one or both parents.
- If no child fits that description, answer about the youngest autistic child.

This rule is designed to identify children with a clearly dateable developmental change while preventing a parent from choosing among multiple qualifying children after seeing later study questions. It creates a selected-child sample and does not estimate the prevalence of skill-loss regression or new-behavior onset among all autistic children or in the general population.

The broad intake and main parent-report cohort are exploratory. A parent's report that the child has autism is sufficient at Survey 1; a professional diagnosis or diagnostic record is not an intake eligibility requirement. Diagnostic history and any available supporting documentation may be collected in the smaller Survey 2/records subset and reported by evidence status. Lack of diagnostic documentation will not by itself discard a submitted case, and parent-reported cases will not be described as clinically verified.

### Onset-age rule for detailed follow-up

Survey 2 and the records phase will invite only otherwise eligible respondents who report that the child's first definite developmental change began at **6 through 24 completed months of age, inclusive**. The age rule uses the first definite developmental change (Onset 2), not an earlier baseline break (Onset 1), vaccination date, diagnosis date, or date of full regression. Survey 1 will still retain and count responses outside the range or with uncertain onset age, but those respondents will not enter the Survey 2 invitation pool. The survey will record age in completed months rather than rounding to the nearest month. Survey 2 will collect the child's birthdate and the reported first-change date so age and boundary discrepancies can be flagged and reported. The 100-record operational target is based on eligibility as screened in Survey 1: a later discrepancy in Survey 2's parent-entered dates or in reviewer-assessed timing will not retroactively remove an otherwise objectively complete submission from that count. Such cases will be retained and reported separately, but will not enter the 6–24-month primary timing analysis if the final age cannot be established in range. Payment remains based on the previously specified good-faith submission rules, not final age classification. The age range will not be widened after examining response yields or timing results.

## 4. Key definitions

### Previously progressing normally

The child had acquired and consistently demonstrated age-appropriate skills or behaviors before the reported change, without a known earlier pattern of developmental regression. The final instrument will collect concrete examples of the child's prior developmental baseline rather than relying only on the parent's category selection. A newly appearing behavior does not require a simultaneous loss of a previously acquired skill.

### Sudden-onset regressive autism (SORA)

A clear change in behavior or skills associated with the child's later autism presentation, first noticeable on an identifiable date by one or both parents after a period of apparently normal development. Either loss or substantial reduction of a previously acquired skill, or the clear new appearance of a marked behavior without skill loss, can qualify. For example, the first noticeable onset of head banging can qualify as a new-behavior presentation. Eligibility does not require the change to unfold or become fully recognizable within seven days.

The study will code skill loss/reduction and new-behavior onset as separate, nonexclusive flags, then report mutually exclusive loss-only, new-behavior-only, and mixed groups separately as well as together. The broader study label does not imply that every qualifying child experienced loss of an acquired skill; conventional skill-loss regression will not be inferred from a new behavior alone.

For this protocol, “sudden onset” means that the parent can identify when the first clear change became noticeable. It does not mean that every affected behavior changed on one day or that the full developmental change was completed within a fixed number of days.

Examples of relevant domains include language, social engagement, eye contact, play, motor abilities, adaptive skills, and the appearance of marked repetitive behavior or sensory disturbance. The survey will collect the concrete observation rather than asking the parent to make a clinical judgment that a behavior was intrinsically autistic. A temporary change during an illness that fully resolves is not, by itself, a qualifying developmental change.

### Parent-observed first definite developmental change

The identifiable date on which one or both parents first observed the qualifying persistent loss/reduction of a skill or marked new behavior. This is distinct from the baseline-break date, the date of diagnosis, the date a clinician documented a concern, and the date the full developmental change was complete.

### Baseline-break date and first definite developmental-change date

The **baseline-break date** is the first dated day on which the child stopped displaying the previously normal baseline and entered a continuous episode that led directly to the later persistent developmental change. The episode may begin with an acute illness, lethargy, prolonged distress, or another condition that prevents reliable assessment of the child's usual skills. The baseline-break date is the study's **primary timing anchor**, but is not automatically the date on which autism-related behavior or skill loss first appeared. The **first definite developmental-change date** is when a parent first clearly noticed the qualifying persistent loss/reduction of a skill or marked new behavior. It is the **secondary timing anchor** and retains the study's parent-observed phenotype definition. Record the last clearly normal observation separately; do not substitute its date for the first day of the break. There is no fixed maximum duration for an adjacent continuous episode. Whether the child fully returned to prior normal functioning is the parent's assessment, recorded explicitly; the study will not impose an arbitrary minimum duration of normal functioning. A parent-reported full return between an earlier uncertain period and the definite developmental change breaks adjacency, regardless of duration. Reviewers will assess the reported sequence and its evidence tier, flag material contradictions, and require adequate date support for objectively verified timing analyses, but will not treat the absence of a separate record proving no return as proof that a return occurred. If no separate adjacent episode is identified, the two dates may be the same. If the start of the break cannot be dated, record it as unresolved rather than assigning the vaccination date or another convenient anchor. The calendar-day gap between these two accepted dates is the **transition gap** used in the paired lag identity; it is not automatically the same as the broader plausible-onset interval defined below.

For accessible figures, **Onset 1 (break from normal)** and **Onset 2 (first definite developmental change)** may be used as short display labels. The full definitions and distinct date fields above remain controlling. In particular, Onset 1 may begin with fever or another period of uncertain functioning and is not, by its name alone, established autism onset.

### Onset uncertainty interval

For loss or reduction of an established skill, the interval between the last confirmed presence of that skill and the first confirmed persistent loss or substantial reduction. For a newly appearing behavior, the interval between the last adequate observation of the child's prior baseline without that behavior and the first definite observation of the new behavior. If the earlier bound cannot be established, it will be recorded as unknown rather than assumed to be the day before the first observation.

Periods in which the child's usual behavior could not be assessed—for example, because of fever, lethargy, hospitalization, or prolonged distress—may widen this interval. The date such a continuous episode began may supply a baseline-break date if it meets the adjacency and evidence rules above. Fever or illness is recorded separately and is not itself defined as regression.

### Objectively complete record submission

A submission from a respondent screened as age-eligible in Survey 1, with all required Survey 2 fields, including the 15-day narrative, the separate pre-disclosure continuity answer, and parent-entered child birthdate, plus documentation of the reported most recent vaccination or vaccine-free regularly scheduled well-child visit within 90 days before the parent-reported first definite developmental change. This is the administrative definition for the 100 documented-visit recruitment target, not the definition of a complete compensated response or a guarantee of 100 age-eligible analytic cases. Administrative staff check that the fields and an apparently in-window visit document are present; the three reviewers do not decide whether a submission counts toward the recruitment target. A later Survey 2 age discrepancy or reviewer change to either timing date does not retroactively change this administrative count. Documentary proof of the child's birthdate is not required. The final records manual will separately define scientific usability and eligibility for each analysis.

## 5. Study flow

### Stage 1: Survey 1—neutral intake

Survey 1 will be a brief developmental-pattern screener. It will collect:

- Consent and confirmation that the respondent is an adult parent or legal guardian.
- The prespecified one-child selection.
- Child birth year.
- Broad developmental pattern.
- For possible identifiable-onset cases: age at the first definite developmental change in completed months (for the 6–24-month follow-up screen), confidence that a specific onset date is known or can be determined later, confidence that relevant records or dated evidence are obtainable, and permission for follow-up. Survey 1 does not require entry or proof of the onset date; Survey 2 asks for it.

Survey 1 will not ask about vaccination status, vaccine products, vaccination dates, or the parent's belief about vaccination. Its purpose is to establish the intake cohort, identify the target phenotype, obtain permission for a second survey, and assign eligible respondents to prespecified record-availability confidence groups.

Survey 1 consent will cover this first-stage intake and optional permission to be contacted about a later stage, not advance agreement to answer that stage's questions or provide records. It will accurately describe the developmental-history procedures, data use, privacy risks, and voluntary nature of Survey 1 without naming particular candidate events. The exact language must be approved by the IRB. Participants can decline the later stage when its additional purpose and procedures are disclosed. Stage-specific nonparticipation will be measured, but a person's reason for stopping will not be inferred from the timing alone.

### Survey 2 invitation phases

Eligible respondents will be assigned to one of four invitation groups using their Survey 1 response about confidence that relevant records or dated evidence are obtainable:

1. **Definitely available now.**
2. **Probably available; I know how to obtain them.**
3. **Might be available; I would need to investigate.**
4. **Probably unavailable.**

These response choices and their order will be fixed before Survey 1 responses are examined. There will not be a fifth “definitely unavailable” invitation group.

1. Draw an initial random batch of 100 eligible respondents from Group 1 and invite them to Survey 2. If Group 1 contains fewer than 100 eligible respondents, invite all of Group 1 and randomly select the balance from Group 2.
2. After the two-week response window, record the number invited and the number of objectively complete record submissions received from invitations whose response windows have closed. No reviewer assessment is needed for this count.
3. If the study remains below the 100-submission target, calculate the next invitation-batch size as `ceiling(1.20 × (100 − C) ÷ (C_m ÷ I_m))`, where `C` is the total number of objectively complete submissions received, `I_m` is the number of invitations whose two-week response windows have closed, and `C_m` is the number of objectively complete submissions from those matured invitations. This uses the observed complete-submission yield per invitation and a 20% allowance so the expected cumulative yield exceeds 100. Do not count an open invitation batch in the denominator.
4. Select each later batch randomly from the highest-confidence group that still contains uninvited eligible respondents. Exhaust a higher-confidence group before drawing from the next group.
5. Continue through Groups 1, 2, 3, and 4 in order until 100 objectively complete record submissions are received or all eligible consenting respondents have been invited.

Each invitation batch will have a two-week response window for the initial operational calculation. If `C_m = 0`, the yield rate cannot be estimated; invite another random batch of 100, or all remaining eligible respondents if fewer than 100 remain, then recalculate after its two-week window. Cap every calculated batch at the number of uninvited eligible respondents available. If a batch exceeds the number remaining in the current confidence group, fill the balance from the next group in order. The random-selection method and seed will be fixed before the first Survey 2 invitation is released. Batch-size calculations may use only operational invitation and objectively complete submission counts—not reviewer judgments, vaccination status, exposure timing, interim histograms, or whether records support a hypothesis.

Wave assignment and the decision to open another wave will not depend on vaccination status, vaccine timing, vaccine product, causal belief, reviewer judgments, or interim timing-analysis results. If more than 100 complete submissions are received because an invitation batch is already open, all submissions will be retained and reported; records will not be discarded to force the analytic sample to exactly 100.

### Stage 2: Survey 2—unprompted reconstruction followed by structured questions

Survey 2 will first ask the parent, without naming vaccination or any other candidate event in the invitation, information and consent materials, or survey questions shown so far, to describe the sequence of events beginning 15 days before the first definite developmental change and ending with that change. The narrative should ask for enough concrete detail to help reviewers assess whether the reported change matches the study definition, including what the child could do before the change, what first changed, who noticed it, and how the change developed afterward. After this original narrative is completed and locked, but still before any named-event disclosure or question, Survey 2 will ask two simple cause-neutral timing questions: (1) when the parent first clearly noticed the persistent developmental change; and (2) whether there had been any earlier period when the parent could not tell whether the child was functioning normally, even if the child later seemed fully normal again. If there were several, use the most recent such period before the clear change. Ask approximately when it began and ended, whether and when the parent judged that the child returned fully to the prior normal baseline, and how many complete normal days passed before the clear change. Do not impose a 15-day or 90-day lookback limit on this question, force an exact date from an approximate duration, or name vaccination or a suggested cause.

The survey platform will display one question at a time. The unprompted narrative and the separate cause-neutral continuity question must be completed and saved before the participant can see any vaccination disclosure or named-event questions. The original narrative will be locked against later edits. After these responses, the participant will receive the IRB-approved additional disclosure explaining that the study examines the timing of medical and other events, including vaccination, and may request relevant records. The participant may decline to continue; no event-specific questions or records request will follow without their affirmative agreement. For those who agree, Survey 2 will then ask for a separate chronological account around the relevant visit and up to the clear change, followed by structured questions about illness, fever, medications, pediatric visits, vaccination, anesthesia, environmental changes, and other candidate exposures. The later account and answers will not alter the locked original narrative.

Delaying the first vaccination disclosure until after the narrative is a fixed design requirement intended to avoid priming recall. The study will seek an explicit IRB determination on whether this sequence requires an alteration of informed consent and, if so, approval of the alteration and any subsequent disclosure. The study will not implement the sequence without that approval; if it is not approved, the protocol must be reconsidered rather than moving vaccination disclosure before the narrative without a study-team decision. The system will log narrative completion, display of the additional disclosure, affirmative continuation, explicit decline, and departure without a recorded choice as distinct states. Use, retention, and any public release of a pre-disclosure narrative from a participant who does not continue must be addressed expressly in the approved protocol and first-stage consent.

Survey 2 will collect or confirm:

- Whether the first clear change involved loss or substantial reduction of a previously acquired skill, a clearly new behavior, or both.
- Separate first-clear-change-day descriptions of each new behavior and each previously demonstrated skill or behavior that was reduced or absent, including explicit none/unknown answers, prior baseline, observer, and persistence.
- For skill loss or reduction: the specific prior skill, its last confirmed presence, and its first confirmed persistent loss or substantial reduction.
- For a new behavior: a concrete description and examples (such as head banging), the last adequate observation of the prior baseline without it when known, and its first definite observation.
- Parent-observed first definite developmental-change date, with any plausible-date bounds recorded separately.
- Whether any uncertain-functioning period occurred before the first definite developmental change; if so, describe the most recent one, its approximate start and end when known, whether full normal functioning returned in the parent's judgment, and the number of complete normal days before the clear change. Record unknown dates or duration rather than infer them from vaccination.
- The onset uncertainty interval.
- Persistence of the change.
- Parent-reported professional autism diagnosis status and, if known, the diagnosing clinician type and approximate diagnosis date; diagnostic documentation may be submitted if available but is not required for participation.
- The unprompted 15-day event narrative.
- A separate post-disclosure chronological account of the days immediately before the most recent pre-change vaccination or applicable vaccine-free visit, the visit day, and notable events until the first clear change, with approximate dates or order when recalled and unknown intervals preserved.
- Records or dated materials believed to be available.
- Permission to be contacted for the records phase.
- The date or age of the most recent regularly scheduled well-child visit within 90 days before the first definite developmental change, if any.
- Whether any vaccine was actually administered at that visit: Yes, No, or Unknown.
- Whether any vaccination occurred after that well-child visit but before the first definite developmental change: Yes, No, or Unknown.
- For a reported vaccination visit before the first definite developmental change: whether any vaccine was given after that visit but before the first definite change: Yes, No, or Unsure. If Yes, ask for the date and products of the later, most recent vaccination visit and request its record. Do not discard the visit if it followed the baseline break.
- For the most recent pre-onset vaccination visit: whether the parent understood the child to be following the routine recommended vaccination schedule at that time, a delayed or alternative plan, or was unsure. This is a parent-reported description, not a determination of adherence to a particular year's schedule.
- For that vaccination visit: whether illness or anything unusual was present in the days beforehand, whether the child was at their usual behavior-and-skill baseline immediately before administration, and the last definitely usual observation if not.
- For every child with a reported pre-onset vaccination, regardless of schedule category: the date of the immediately preceding vaccination visit, if known; otherwise an approximate interval since that visit, "no earlier vaccination," or "don't know." Count visits on distinct dates, not the number of vaccine products given at one visit. Calculate the spacing from two dates when available. The preceding visit need not be documented to answer this survey question; mark its date or interval as parent-reported unless independently supported.
- Whether the child has ever received any vaccination, anywhere and to the parent's knowledge: Yes, No, or Unsure. A No answer defines **parent-reported never vaccinated** without mandatory documentary proof; it is separate from whether any vaccination occurred within 90 days before the first definite developmental change.
- Whether any regularly scheduled well-child visit occurred within 90 days before the first definite developmental change: Yes, No, or Unsure. If neither a vaccination nor a well-child visit occurred in that period, record both absences rather than forcing a visit date.
- After the named-event disclosure, ask whether fever, malaise, unusual crying, lethargy, or another acute symptom followed a reported vaccination before the first definite developmental change. If so, ask when symptoms began and how long after vaccination the last such symptom ended, including same-day, still ongoing at the clear change, approximate, and unknown answers. Separately ask whether the child fully returned to prior normal functioning and, if so, the directly reported number of consecutive complete normal days immediately before the definite change. Preserve no full return and a return shorter than one complete day as separate zero-day answers. Do not equate symptom resolution with full developmental recovery or derive normal days only by subtracting two reported lags. This prompted sequence is stored separately from the locked unprompted narrative.

The original unprompted narrative will be saved immediately and preserved as submitted. It may be used, together with the structured before-and-after skill questions, to assess whether the reported event satisfies the onset definition. Later prompts, clarifications, or record review will be stored separately and will not overwrite the original account.

If a participant stops after submitting the narrative but before completing Survey 2, the proposed policy is to retain the narrative and all previously submitted answers as a partial response, including when the participant declines at the additional disclosure. No submitted survey answer will be discarded merely because the participant stops at disclosure; the partial-response status and point of departure will be retained for analysis. The consent presented before any study questions must disclose this proposed partial-response retention, the planned redacted public release described below, and the study's no-revocation policy. This treatment of pre-disclosure narratives requires specific IRB approval. The analysis plan will specify which descriptive or eligibility assessments may use partial responses and which analyses require a completed Survey 2 or usable records.

### Stage 3: records request and validation

Only respondents who meet the prespecified study eligibility definition will enter the Survey 2 invitation pool. Within that eligible pool, invitation-wave assignment will depend only on the respondent's Survey 1 confidence that relevant records or dated evidence can be produced. It will not depend on onset-date precision, vaccination status, reported proximity to vaccination, vaccine product, the parent's causal belief, or whether the record is expected to support a hypothesis. Vaccination status is not collected in Survey 1 and is therefore unknown when wave assignment is made.

The study will seek at least 100 objectively complete record submissions from respondents screened as eligible for the 6–24-completed-month onset-age cohort. This count includes submissions with documentation of the most recent vaccination before the parent-reported first definite developmental change and submissions with documentation of a vaccine-free regularly scheduled well-child visit before that change. Administrative completeness is determined by the presence of the required survey fields and documents, before the three reviewers assess either timing date or decide which records qualify for each analysis. If the first participating source does not yield 100 complete submissions, recruitment may continue sequentially through as many as three additional approved sources. Every submission received from an open invitation batch will be retained and reviewed, even if the total exceeds 100. The study will report the number of submitted records that are scientifically usable after review, including later age-boundary discrepancies; reviewer decisions will not change the recruitment threshold or trigger additional invitations.

Parents who make a good-faith submission of the requested materials by the stated deadline will receive a $25 Amazon gift card under IRB-approved terms. The $25 payment will be issued even if the submitted material is incomplete, cannot be used in the final analysis, establishes a vaccine-free visit, contradicts the parent's recollection, or does not support any study hypothesis.

### Adaptive compensation if record yield is inadequate

The IRB submission will request advance approval for the following staged compensation schedule for a complete response:

1. Begin at $25.
2. At each two-week operational checkpoint, determine whether 100 objectively complete record submissions have been received.
3. If the target has not been reached, increase compensation by $25 for a complete response: first to $50, then to $75, and finally to the maximum of $100.
4. Stop increasing compensation once 100 objectively complete record submissions have been received or the $100 cap has been reached.

The first two-week checkpoint will run from the date the first Survey 2 invitation wave is released. Each later checkpoint will occur two weeks after the preceding increase. A response qualifies for the full amount then offered when the participant has submitted all required Survey 2 fields, including the 15-day narrative and birthdate, plus at least one requested evidence item: documentation of an in-window vaccination or well-child visit, or an apparently dateable contemporaneous item with onset-related content. A no-visit family that submits onset evidence therefore qualifies for the same full amount, including any later retroactive top-up. Administrative staff verify receipt, apparent dateability, and presence of onset-related content, not whether the evidence proves the onset date; that scientific judgment belongs to the three reviewers. A good-faith participant without a qualifying evidence item still receives the guaranteed $25. Full-payment eligibility does not depend on reviewer judgments, SORA eligibility, evidentiary strength, vaccination status, inclusion in an analysis, or whether the submission supports a hypothesis.

The 100 documented-visit submission count governs compensation-increase checkpoints and the recruitment stopping rule, but it is separate from individual eligibility for the full payment. No-visit responses with dated onset evidence receive the full payment without being counted as documented-visit submissions. Reaching 100 documented-visit submissions stops compensation increases and further invitation batches; all submitted material still undergoes the prespecified scientific review, and the final number eligible for each analysis will be reported.

The decision to activate a higher amount may use only the count of objectively complete submissions received by the checkpoint. It may not use vaccination status, exposure timing, reviewer judgments, scientific usability, interim histograms, or whether received records support a hypothesis.

When compensation is increased, every earlier participant who completed the same required activities will be offered a retroactive top-up sufficient to bring that participant's total compensation to the new amount. Thus, participants completing equivalent work will ultimately receive equal compensation regardless of enrollment date. The consent and recruitment materials will state the payment schedule, objective completion requirements, timing and method of payment, retroactive top-up rule, and that payment is compensation for time and effort rather than a study benefit.

No payment may depend on a particular answer, vaccination history, reviewer judgment, eligibility for the primary analysis, or agreement with the study hypothesis. The evidence alternatives reflect which records exist, not a preferred answer. A participant who makes a good-faith submission but cannot produce a qualifying evidence item will retain the guaranteed $25 payment.

The records request will be deliberately narrow. It is expected to include:

- The completed unprompted narrative covering the 15 days before the first definite developmental change through that change, plus the separate pre-disclosure continuity answer if a baseline break began earlier.
- The child's birthdate as entered by the parent; separate documentary proof is not required.
- If the parent reports vaccination within 90 days before the first definite developmental change: a record showing the actual date and products of the most recent vaccination in that window. If the parent reports a later vaccination before that change, request the record for the later visit instead, even if it followed the baseline break.
- If the parent reports no vaccination within 90 days before the first definite developmental change: documentation of the most recent regularly scheduled well-child visit without vaccination in that same window, if any, including the visit date and, when available, the visit note or immunization record supporting that no vaccine was administered.
- Any available contemporaneous materials that can corroborate or bound the reported onset, such as a pediatrician's note, text or email, existing lawfully recorded voicemail or call, contemporaneous call note, diary entry, dated video or photograph, daycare record, or calendar entry. These will be actively requested. They are not required for the 100 documented-visit administrative count, but an apparently dateable item with relevant onset content can satisfy the full-payment evidence requirement when no visit document exists. A call log showing only that a call occurred, without contemporaneous evidence of what was said, does not by itself corroborate onset. Absence of corroboration will be reported and may prevent inclusion in the primary timing analysis.
- Any available dated material documenting the start, duration, or resolution of a continuous period in which the child was not clearly at the prior baseline before the definite developmental change. This may support the baseline-break date but will not be required for the 100 documented-visit administrative count.

If the parent reports neither vaccination nor a regularly scheduled well-child visit within 90 days before the first definite developmental change, no visit document will be requested merely to manufacture an anchor. The study will still retain the survey response and request dated contemporaneous timing evidence; submission of such an item can satisfy the full-payment evidence requirement even though the case has no documented visit and does not count toward the 100 documented-visit target. It will separately record whether the child was reported never vaccinated, was vaccinated only outside the 90-day window, or has an unknown lifetime vaccination history. A parent report of never vaccinated is sufficient for inclusion in the explicitly labeled parent-reported subgroup; absence of a visit document is not proof of never-vaccinated status. If an immunization-registry extract or sufficiently complete clinical history is voluntarily available, its concordance with the parent report will be recorded as a separate corroboration status, not made a condition of participation or payment.

The study will not require the parent to submit the child's complete vaccination history. The vaccination evidence must reflect actual administration rather than a scheduled appointment or routine age recommendation. If the parent reports no later vaccination after the submitted visit, the analysis will identify that visit as the parent-confirmed most recent vaccination only if it falls within the 90 days before the first definite developmental change. If the parent is unsure, the submission remains in the dataset but its most-recent-vaccination interval is flagged as unconfirmed. If the parent reports a later vaccination before the first definite change, the earlier visit will not be treated as most recent; the later visit's date, products, and supporting record will be requested. A vaccination or well-child visit outside that 90-day window may be retained as context but cannot serve as a visit anchor. The records manual will define how to handle reports of no vaccination before the definite change and missing or conflicting visit records.

The reviewers—not the parent—will independently extract and assess the key dates and their supporting evidence in one review of all available material. Study staff will then apply prespecified consistency and dataset-construction rules. Parent-reported onset, each reviewer's evidence-informed estimate, and the values accepted by at least two reviewers will remain separate variables.

### One-pass, three-reviewer assessment

The three designated reviewers—Steve Kirsch, Karl Jablonowski, and Brian Hooker—will each independently evaluate every candidate record once, using the same prespecified rubric and all available material: the original unprompted narrative, later Survey 2 answers, uploaded videos, photographs, messages, clinical records, vaccination records, and other evidence. Each reviewer will decide whether the case meets the study's SORA definition (Yes, No, or Uncertain) and complete the date-and-evidence form below. Vaccination information will not be masked. “Independently” means that each reviewer completes, timestamps, and locks an assessment without seeing or discussing either other reviewer's assessment; it does not mean that the reviewers are independent of the study team. If no evidence beyond the parent's narrative supports onset, that fact will be flagged and the case will not be represented as objectively corroborated onset.

For each case, every reviewer will independently complete the same date-and-evidence form:

1. Child's birthdate as entered by the parent, with any discrepancy noted. This is a parent-reported date, not a document-verified date unless supporting evidence happens to be available; separate birthdate proof is not required.
2. The actual date of the most recent documented vaccination within 90 days before the first definite developmental-change date, and every product given on that date; or **none established / unknown**. The reviewer will identify the administration record, not infer a dose from the recommended schedule or a planned visit. This visit may be before or after the baseline-break date; its chronological relation to each date will be preserved.
3. Only if no vaccination occurred in that 90-day period before the first definite developmental change, the date of the most recent regularly scheduled well-child visit without shots **within the same 90-day period**, and the evidence for its date and vaccine-free status; or **no qualifying visit / unknown**. A vaccine-free visit alone does not establish absence of vaccination at another visit; the reviewer will separately code whether the 90-day vaccination history is confirmed complete, parent-reported only, or unknown. A no-visit case remains reviewable for phenotype and timing.
4. Two distinct dates: the **baseline-break date**, meaning the first day of a continuous departure from the prior normal baseline that directly precedes the lasting developmental change, and the **first definite developmental-change date**, meaning when the parent first clearly noticed the qualifying persistent loss/reduction or marked new behavior. The reviewer will also record the parent's most recent earlier uncertain-functioning period, its approximate duration, any full return to prior normal functioning, and the number of complete normal days before the definite change, together with supporting or conflicting evidence. This earlier period need not be adjacent to the change and must not automatically supply the baseline-break date. No fixed transition-gap cutoff will substitute for the adjacency assessment. The reviewer will not choose the vaccination date as the baseline-break date merely because vaccination occurred then. Separately, record the **earliest documented post-change date**: the first date on which submitted contemporaneous evidence actually documents the qualifying changed behavior. These fields may coincide but are never silently substituted for one another.
5. The earliest and latest plausible dates for the qualifying developmental change and their width in days, or **unknown** where a bound cannot be established. These bounds express uncertainty about when the lasting change began; they do not turn either point-date histogram entry into a range. If the date is judged exact, both bounds equal that date and the width is zero. A dated after-change item establishes that the change was present by that date, but without evidence of the prior baseline it does not establish how much earlier the change may have occurred.

Each date or status will have an evidence citation and source classification (contemporaneous document, later parent report, or inference), plus a conflict/insufficiency flag. The 90-day record-request and visit-eligibility classification will use the first definite developmental-change date; baseline-break-to-visit lags may therefore be negative. A 90-day classification will be flagged if the developmental-change uncertainty bounds straddle the cutoff. Objective corroboration of each timing date will be an explicitly reported scientific evidence tier, separate from whether the submission is administratively complete and counts toward the 100-record recruitment threshold.

Onset evidence qualifies by its contemporaneous content and credible date, not by medium. A pediatrician's record, text message, voicemail or recorded call, contemporaneous call note, diary entry, or similar item may establish that a specific change was first noticed on a stated date or bound the interval in which it became noticeable. A single credibly dated after-change video showing the relevant behavior also qualifies as objective onset-related evidence; a before-video or other pre-change item is not required to accept the video as evidence. Its date is the earliest **documented post-change observation** if no earlier qualifying item exists. It proves that the behavior was present by that date, not necessarily that it first began on that date. If the video itself or another contemporaneous item records that the change first appeared that day, the reviewer may additionally assess that as support for a same-day onset date. Reviewers will record both when an item was created and the date of onset it describes; a later clinical note repeating an old parental recollection is not itself contemporaneous corroboration of that earlier date. A bare appointment entry, call log, or undated image establishes at most that an encounter or file exists, not when the developmental change began. The manual will prespecify how reviewers evaluate original-versus-copy provenance, timestamps, alterations, conflicting accounts, and before/after evidence. No single format is automatically accepted or rejected; reviewers must explain how the item's actual content supports the selected onset date or bounds.

The primary baseline-break timing analysis will require a first definite developmental-change age of 6 through 24 completed months, calculated from the parent-entered birthdate and reviewer-agreed first definite developmental-change date; reviewer-agreed dates for both the baseline break and first definite developmental change; dateable evidence supporting the start or continuity of the break and the later developmental change; plus documentation of the actual vaccination administration date and products or of the qualifying vaccine-free well visit and sufficiently established 90-day vaccination history. The developmental-change uncertainty bounds will remain separate variables. The records manual will prespecify what counts as corroboration and adequate precision before review begins. Cases later judged outside the age range or with unresolved age remain retained and reported separately rather than being silently discarded. Cases with an unresolved baseline-break date may still enter the separate first-definite-change analysis if the age rule can be established; cases supported only by retrospective parent narrative, or with uncertain vaccination history, remain in descriptive and sensitivity analyses as appropriate but will not be presented as objectively verified primary timing cases.

Each reviewer will record the evidence supporting and contradicting the judgment. All three assessments will be locked before any reviewer sees the others' judgments. Once locked, a judgment may not be revised to create agreement or in response to an interim timing-analysis result.

**Majority-agreement rule:** After all three assessments are locked, a SORA eligibility judgment, either timing anchor, any other date-form value or status, the separately recorded first documented post-change date, or each numerical developmental-change bound is accepted for subsequent analysis only when at least two reviewers independently agree on that exact judgment or number. Each bound is assessed separately, and its interval width is calculated from the agreed bounds rather than voted on independently. Agreed lower and upper bounds may form an analysis interval only when they are internally consistent; otherwise the interval remains unresolved. The study will not average disparate dates, invent a compromise number, or change a reviewer's locked answer to manufacture agreement. All three original assessments remain in the audit trail. A case without the agreed values required for a particular analysis is retained and reported but excluded from that analysis.

**Analysis dataset construction:** After all three assessments and the majority-agreement determinations are locked, study staff will preserve three separate date fields for each case: baseline break, first definite developmental change, and earliest documented post-change observation. Each reviewer supplies one date or an unresolved status for each applicable field; an accepted date is unresolved if fewer than two reviewers choose the same calendar date. No field will be filled from another except that a reviewer may explicitly judge the baseline break and definite change to have occurred on the same day. The first definite developmental-change date will define the common visit-ascertainment window for the paired histograms: link it and the birthdate to at most one visit anchor, (1) the most recent documented vaccination within 90 days before that date, with its products; otherwise (2) the most recent documented vaccine-free regularly scheduled well-child visit within those same 90 days, provided no vaccination occurred in the window. Then calculate a separate lag from that same visit to each accepted timing date. A visit between the baseline break and definite developmental change remains in the dataset and yields a negative baseline-break lag; it is not described as a pre-baseline-break exposure. Cases missing either date remain in the dataset and are reported separately; the directly paired display includes only cases with both dates and the required visit record. If neither visit exists or the necessary vaccination history is unknown, there is no qualifying visit anchor; the case remains in the dataset with its phenotype, timing information, and evidence tier. Reviewer classifications and source evidence will remain in the dataset. This subsequent dataset step does not mean that the reviewers were blind to vaccination information.

Because reviewers see vaccination information in their single assessment, the onset judgments are not exposure-blinded. Independent voting can measure disagreement but cannot eliminate a shared influence of exposure information on onset dating. The study will report the evidence supporting each accepted value and the rates of agreement and unresolved judgments.

The study will report:

- Agreement among the three reviewers on SORA eligibility.
- Agreement among the three reviewer-derived onset dates or bounds.
- The number and proportion of cases with exact three-reviewer agreement.
- The number and proportion with exact two-of-three agreement on each required judgment or value, and the numbers left unresolved with reasons for disagreement.
- The number and proportion of original narratives that spontaneously mention vaccination, and the evidence tier supporting each accepted onset value.

Exact agreement among all three reviewers is the preferred outcome, but two matching independent assessments are sufficient for a value to survive into analysis. Disagreement will be preserved rather than forced into agreement. The validation manual will define the evidence-access workflow, exact-value and interval-bound formats, agreement metrics, consistency checks, evidence tiers, and handling of unresolved fields before review begins. Accepted values will be selected without the reviewers seeing one another's judgments, but with vaccination information available to each reviewer.

## 6. Protection against hypothesis-driven selection

The central design safeguard is the separation of recruitment and records selection from vaccination ascertainment.

Before vaccination information can influence any invitation or record-request decision, the study will freeze and retain an auditable record of:

1. Survey 1 eligibility.
2. Record-availability assessment.
3. Survey 2 invitation group and the reason for its assignment.
4. The date each invitation group was opened.
5. Whether Survey 2 and records invitations were sent.

The study team will not use vaccination status or timing to choose whom to invite, whom to remind, which submissions count toward the 100-record administrative target, or which good-faith submissions to compensate. The later scientific phenotype and onset judgments will be made by the three reviewers with all available evidence, including vaccination information. The study will not describe those judgments as exposure-blinded.

This design reduces selection based on the exposure of interest. It does not eliminate nonresponse, inaccurate recall, incomplete records, differential willingness to participate, or selection associated with membership-organization or clinic recruitment. Those limitations will be measured and reported by recruitment source where possible.

- Original parent-reported first definite developmental-change date and confidence.
- Original unprompted narrative, narrative-based onset assessment, and later structured event responses.
- A separately stored, post-disclosure chronological account from the days immediately before the most recent pre-change vaccination (or applicable nonvaccination anchor) through the first clear developmental change, including event order and approximate dates when recalled. The original unprompted narrative remains locked and distinct.
- Three independent evidence-informed assessments, agreement measures, and the separately identified values accepted by at least two reviewers.
- Phenotype flags for skill loss/reduction and new-behavior onset, including cases with both.
- Separate descriptions of behaviors first appearing on the first clear-change day and previously demonstrated skills or behaviors reduced or absent on that day, with none/unknown responses, prior baseline, observer, and persistence recorded.
- Last-confirmed-skill-presence and first-confirmed-loss bounds, or last-observed-baseline-without-new-behavior and first-observed-new-behavior bounds, as applicable.
- Developmental-change uncertainty interval, last clearly normal observation, continuous baseline-break episode start and end, intervening full-return-to-baseline status, and episode description.
- For the most recent parent-reported uncertain-functioning period before the definite change, its start and end, cause-neutral description, full-return status, and the number of complete normal days before the definite change (X), with uncertainty and evidence tier. Vaccination, fever, or another candidate event may be linked to the period only in later prompted data; X itself does not require a vaccination. Keep X separate from the transition gap of a continuous baseline-break episode.
- For a vaccination before the clear change, parent-reported illness or other unusual symptoms in the immediately preceding days, whether usual behavior and skills were clearly present immediately before administration, and the last definitely usual observation if not. Record post-vaccination acute-symptom onset and end separately from any full developmental return to baseline.
- Directly reported complete normal days immediately before the definite change following a post-vaccination symptom period, with no-return, less-than-one-complete-day return, positive-day, unknown, and no-post-vaccination-symptom categories kept distinct. Retain the symptom-resolution lag, direct normal-day answer, and the cause-neutral pre-disclosure X response as separate fields rather than deriving or overwriting one from another.
- Record availability, submission, usability, and evidence tier.
- Three separate fields for every case: agreed baseline-break date, agreed first definite developmental-change date, and agreed earliest documented post-change date, each with its own source, agreement status, and unresolved/missing value where applicable; developmental-change bounds remain separate.
- Derived transition gap, baseline-break lag, and definite-change lag where their required dates are available; keep these distinct from the broader plausible-onset uncertainty interval and the separate vaccination-to-documentation lag.
- Day of week of each reviewer-agreed baseline-break date, derived from the calendar date rather than asked of the parent, with missing or date-uncertain cases counted separately; retain the vaccination weekday for context.
- Each reviewer's birthdate, most recent documented vaccination date within 90 days before the first definite developmental change (if any), last eligible vaccine-free well-visit date (if applicable), both timing-anchor dates and developmental-change bounds, source citations, evidence tier, and unresolved/conflict flags.
- Actual date and products for the documented most recent vaccination before the first definite developmental change, including its position before or after the baseline break.
- Whether the parent reported a later vaccination between the submitted vaccination visit and the first definite developmental change: Yes, No, or Unsure; when Yes, the later visit's date, products, and record status.
- Parent-reported routine versus delayed/alternative vaccination schedule at the most recent visit before the first definite change, and the reported date or approximate spacing of the immediately preceding vaccination visit, with no-earlier-visit, unknown, and corroboration statuses. These fields will permit a descriptive check of how often more than one vaccination visit fell within the 90 days before the first definite change; an undated or unsupported preceding visit will not be treated as a verified exposure date.
- Whether no prior vaccination was reported, the most recent vaccination could not be established, or the supporting record was insufficient.
- Date of the most recent regularly scheduled well-child visit within 90 days before the first definite change, whether vaccination was actually administered at that visit, and whether any vaccination occurred in the 90-day window.
- Separate flags for parent-reported never vaccinated, no vaccination in the 90-day window, no regularly scheduled well-child visit in that window, unknown history, and optional registry/clinical-history corroboration of lifetime status; no-anchor cases will not be silently excluded.
- Other dated acute events and relevant medical encounters.

Absolute dates will be used only in the restricted study environment as required for validation and interval calculation. The public-use dataset will use derived ages and intervals and will exclude direct identifiers and other fields that create unacceptable re-identification risk.

## 8. Analysis framework

The statistical analysis plan will be finalized, simulated, and frozen before vaccination records are examined for the main records cohort.

### Descriptive analyses

- Recruitment and attrition at every study stage.
- Continuation and drop-off at the prespecified point when vaccination is first disclosed, with the denominator of participants who reached that point and no assumption about why an individual stopped.
- Developmental-pattern distribution among Survey 1 respondents.
- Separate counts and timing summaries for skill-loss/reduction, new-behavior-only, and mixed presentations.
- Parent-reported age-at-onset histogram.
- Distribution of onset-date confidence and onset uncertainty.
- Record availability, retrieval, usability, and agreement with parent reports.
- Frequencies and timing of unprompted and prompted candidate events.

### Records-based timing analyses

The single planned primary day-0–2 timing endpoint and primary histogram use the reviewers' agreed **baseline-break date**: the first dated interruption of normal baseline in a continuous episode adjacent to and leading to the definite developmental change. The **definite-change lag** is the prespecified secondary endpoint and required companion histogram, not a co-primary test. Both metrics are required displays for the overall vaccinated group and every plotted visit-age and vaccine-product subgroup. The **earliest documented post-change date** remains a third, separately labeled documentation-date analysis, not another onset estimate. Every plot, table, and reported lag will identify its date basis, eligibility count, and denominator; no date will silently substitute for an unresolved date. For a directly comparable pair, the paired histograms will use the same children with both reviewer-agreed dates and a qualifying vaccination record. Available-case displays may additionally show children with only one accepted date, with different denominators plainly marked. The statistical analysis plan will lock the paired-cohort and available-case rules before exposure-linked results are examined. Adjacency and full return to baseline will be judged from the clinical narrative and records under a prespecified rubric, not inferred from proximity to vaccination. There is no fixed maximum transition gap for the primary paired analysis. Report the observed transition-gap distribution and repeat the paired timing analyses as a prespecified sensitivity analysis restricted to gaps of 30 days or less; report cases over 30 days separately, without removing otherwise eligible cases from the primary analysis.

For each included child, calculate two integer day lags from the same documented vaccination date: **baseline-break lag = baseline-break date − vaccination date** and **definite-change lag = first definite developmental-change date − vaccination date** (day 0 means the dates coincide). Define the **transition gap = first definite developmental-change date − baseline-break date**; therefore **baseline-break lag = definite-change lag − transition gap**. This is an exact calendar-date identity, not an imputation from an unrelated interval width. Under the rule selecting a vaccination on or before the first definite change, the definite-change lag is nonnegative; the baseline-break lag may be negative. Negative baseline-break lags are valid observations and will be plotted in labeled negative-day bins, not excluded, set to zero, or moved to a different visit. A negative value means vaccination occurred after the baseline break and cannot be called a pre-break exposure. The separate plausible developmental-change bounds can support labeled sensitivity analyses; they will not silently replace either point-date histogram entry. The first documented post-change date yields a distinct vaccination-to-documentation lag.

For participants with an adequate most-recent-vaccination record, analyses may include:

- Paired baseline-break-lag and definite-change-lag histograms for the overall group, using the same documented vaccination date and the same children where both dates are accepted.
- Both lag histograms for last vaccinating visits in each prespecified age-at-administration group centered on 15 and 18 months, using the same age-bin membership for the pair. Other administration ages will be reported rather than silently excluded. The exact nonoverlapping age-group boundaries will be frozen in the statistical analysis plan.
- Both lag histograms for each vaccine product administered at the last vaccinating visit, with product-specific inferential analyses only when sample size and multiplicity control permit. Each child appears at most once in each plot. A child given several products at the last visit may appear once in each applicable product plot as well as in the overall and applicable visit-age plots; those overlapping plots cannot by themselves attribute an association to one product.
- Prespecified day-0–2 and reference-window summaries separately for each date basis, including negative-day baseline-break counts and exact denominators.
- Sensitivity analyses using the earliest and latest plausible onset bounds, including the effect of unresolved or weakly corroborated onset dates.
- Analyses accounting for age, calendar time, vaccination schedule, and multiple products administered at the most recent visit.

### Fever, recovery, and later definite change

As a prespecified secondary sequence analysis among regression cases, report the number with a parent-reported uncertain-functioning period before the first definite developmental change, regardless of vaccination or the cause of that period. A required figure will be **one overall histogram of X**, the number of complete days of prior normal functioning between the most recent such period and the definite change, using labeled integer-day bins over the observed range and no age-group panels or upper cap on X. **X = 0** when the parent reports no full return before the definite change or a full return lasting less than one complete day; retain these as two separately coded reasons for zero. The denominator is all regression cases with an uncertain-functioning period and enough information to assign X, regardless of vaccination or whether a full return occurred. Show the total with the period, the number with each zero reason, positive X, and unknown or insufficient timing, together with evidence tiers. Describe the subset whose uncertain period followed the documented most recent vaccination separately without changing the denominator of the overall X figure. A fully recovered earlier uncertain period is not adjacent to the later change and will not supply Onset 1; the reviewers will assess whether a separate later baseline break is dateable.

For the subset reporting acute symptoms after the documented most recent vaccination and before the clear change, also plot a separately labeled histogram of the **directly reported consecutive complete normal days immediately before the clear change**. Report its own denominator, the no-full-return and less-than-one-complete-day components of zero, positive days, unknown answers, and cases with no post-vaccination symptoms. Summarize the vaccination-to-symptom-end lag separately from vaccination-to-clear-change lag and from the directly reported normal days. When exact dates are available, compare the direct normal-day answer with the date-derived gap after reported full return to baseline; flag differences and partial-day ambiguity rather than forcing the values to agree. Ending fever, crying, lethargy, or other acute symptoms does not by itself establish full return to prior behavior and skills.

This sequence directly probes the proposed continuity pattern: a credible case with X greater than zero would disprove an absolute claim that it never occurs. If a large, well-ascertained eligible group has no X greater than zero, that would be noteworthy and should be reported with its exact denominator and a confidence bound, not dismissed as an insignificant zero. It would not by itself establish a vaccine effect. The overall X histogram requires no age breakdown, but a no-vaccine-effect null does not imply equal counts at every X: timing and duration of uncertain periods, age-dependent change rates, observation windows, and selection of children who later regress affect the expected distribution. A null additionally assuming that the uncertain period and later change have no shared cause is narrower and must be stated and justified separately. A formal test requires a separately specified and calibrated expected distribution, frozen before the relevant data are examined; otherwise the X distribution is descriptive.

For the post-vaccination normal-days histogram, a **zero spike followed by a roughly flat positive tail** is one explicit comparison model, not a prediction of vaccine safety alone. It follows approximately when the age-adjusted clear-change hazard is locally constant within a fixed observation window, the post-vaccination recovery duration is fixed or narrowly distributed, the recovered child stays at prior baseline until an independent clear change, and recruitment, recall, and records selection do not vary across lag days. In that model, the expected zero mass depends on the recovery-duration fraction of the observation window. With varying recovery durations, delayed symptom onset, later interruptions, age or schedule effects, and incomplete day-level information, the positive tail need not be flat. The statistical analysis plan will state and simulate these assumptions, use observed recovery-duration information without treating a parent-labeled post-vaccination symptom as proven vaccine-caused, and report sensitivity to plausible alternatives. Without a defensible calibrated model, interpret the histogram descriptively rather than as a formal vaccine-causation test.

A second required figure will be a seven-bar Monday-through-Sunday histogram of the reviewer-agreed exact Onset 1 date among all detailed cases with that date, with the eligible denominator and missing or date-uncertain counts shown. Display the vaccination weekday for the vaccinated subset as a companion panel so visit scheduling can be inspected. Both weekdays are derived from recorded dates, adding no survey question. This is a descriptive timing and data-quality check, not a causal test; vaccination visits and reported dates may have weekday patterns.

### Calendar-quarter date-anchor histograms

For every baseline-break-lag and definite-change-lag histogram, the study will plot a companion histogram of days from the first day of the calendar quarter containing that same specified date to that date. This applies to both date metrics in the overall last-vaccination group, each prespecified age-at-vaccination group (such as the 15-month and 18-month groups), and each plotted vaccine-product group. Each companion plot will use exactly the children in its corresponding vaccination plot; group membership is determined by the child's documented last vaccinating visit and its products, not by the calendar-quarter anchor. Each child contributes one quarter-start lag to each applicable companion plot, even when several products were given at one visit. The date basis, eligibility count, denominator, and bin definitions will be shown for each display. Quarter-start lags cannot be negative by construction; negative baseline-break lags will remain visible in the paired vaccination plot rather than being removed for symmetry.

The quarter-start lag is an integer number of calendar days. Calendar quarters have different lengths, so the companion display will retain a labeled overflow bin for any quarter-start lags beyond the common 90-day comparison range rather than silently exclude those children; the statistical analysis plan will fix the common range and endpoint convention before the plots are examined. As prespecified sensitivity displays, repeat the quarter-anchor calculation using quarterly grids shifted forward by one calendar month (February/May/August/November starts) and two calendar months (March/June/September/December starts), keeping the same child subsets and date basis. These related calendar anchors are descriptive checks for clustering around arbitrary dates. They are not vaccination events and will not, without a separately calibrated model, be treated as the formal no-vaccination-effect distribution for vaccine lags.

### Child age-quarter anchor histograms

For the prespecified 12–18-completed-month first-definite-change subgroup, add a descriptive companion plot anchored to each child's own three-month age milestones rather than to calendar-quarter starts. Calculate the 12-, 15-, and 18-month dates from the parent-entered birthdate. Select the most recent applicable milestone on or before the reviewer-agreed first definite developmental-change date, then use that **same milestone** for both date metrics: baseline-break date minus age-quarter anchor and first definite developmental-change date minus age-quarter anchor. Use exactly the children in the corresponding documented-vaccination paired plots, show the same eligibility counts and denominators, and preserve negative baseline-break lags. The statistical analysis plan will fix the exact subgroup boundaries, treatment of an onset exactly on a milestone, and any partial final age-quarter interval before results are inspected. This plot checks whether clustering around an actual vaccination date exceeds clustering around the child's three-month age milestone. A vaccine visit need not occur on the milestone date; neither this plot nor the calendar-quarter plot is, by itself, a calibrated no-effect distribution or a causal test. No additional survey question is required because the child's birthdate is already collected.

The two date metrics have different null expectations. A smooth age distribution of first definite developmental change, estimated from Survey 1 under a prespecified method, may inform the secondary histogram's comparison model. It cannot be substituted for the baseline-break distribution: the latter includes acute illness, lethargy, distress, or other interruptions that need not be developmental regression and may themselves occur shortly after vaccination. Thus even under a null of no vaccine effect on autism-related developmental change, the primary baseline-break histogram could show an early post-vaccination spike if vaccination caused transient fever or distress. The study will not add a non-regression comparison cohort or questions for one to Survey 1; this preserves the short intake and the focus of the records phase. The planned primary test therefore concerns the timing of continuous baseline-break episodes among children with a later qualifying developmental change; it cannot by itself test whether vaccination caused that later change. The statistical analysis plan must specify and simulate separate null models, eligibility rules, negative-day handling, and day-0–2 tests for the two metrics. Until a valid baseline-break null is established and frozen before relevant exposure-linked results are examined, its day-0–2 pattern is a prespecified descriptive timing result, not a calibrated inferential test. Product-specific plots will use the same last-visit anchor and explicitly identify coadministered products. Nominal “15-month” or “18-month” visit labels will not replace actual administration dates.

Estimating a smooth curve from Survey 1 reports of first definite developmental change does not by itself prove that curve is a vaccine-free natural history: the observed distribution may include any true exposure effect, recall anchoring, and recruitment or record-selection effects. The final statistical plan must specify separate expected-count calculations for the baseline-break and definite-change metrics, eligible age ranges, day-0 convention, timing uncertainty, handling of later or missed visits, and calibration by simulation. If the available data cannot support a valid expected-count model for either metric, that short-lag result will be reported as a prespecified descriptive clustering measure rather than a calibrated association test.

### Vaccine-free well-visit comparison

The study will retain participants for whom no vaccination was given in the 90 days before the first definite developmental change and who had at least one regularly scheduled well-child visit without vaccination in that same window. For participants whose 90-day vaccination history and well-visit status can be adequately established, it will plot both the baseline-break-to-visit and definite-change-to-visit lags using the same child set and binning as the paired vaccination plots where possible. A well-child visit more than 90 days before the first definite change will not be used as the comparison anchor. A well visit after the baseline break yields a negative baseline-break lag and remains visible.

The primary vaccine-free well-visit comparison group will require:

1. An identifiable regularly scheduled well-child visit within 90 days before the first definite developmental change.
2. Confirmation that no vaccine was administered at that visit.
3. Confirmation that no vaccination occurred in the 90 days before the first definite developmental change and between that visit and that change. A single vaccine-free visit record is insufficient for this classification if other vaccinations could have occurred elsewhere.
4. A documented visit date and reviewer-agreed baseline-break and first-definite-change dates for paired plots, with uncertainty reported separately.

Cases with unknown vaccination-at-visit status, an intervening vaccination, unknown 90-day or intervening-vaccination status, or inadequate dates will be retained and reported but will not be silently classified as confirmed vaccine-free controls. Survey-reported and record-confirmed groups will be shown separately.

Both plots will be produced even if the confirmed group is small. Exact numerators and denominators will accompany them. A sparse comparison will be labeled descriptive or inconclusive rather than treated as evidence of equivalence or no association.

### No-visit-anchor group

Participants with neither vaccination nor a regularly scheduled well-child visit in the 90 days before the first definite developmental change will be retained and reported as a separate group. The study will describe their reported and reviewer-accepted baseline-break and first-definite-change ages, phenotype, date uncertainty, evidence tiers, and reported lifetime vaccination status. Cases reported as never vaccinated will be distinguished from children who were vaccinated earlier but not in the 90-day window and from children with unknown history. Parent report alone is sufficient for the labeled parent-reported never-vaccinated subgroup; any cases with optional registry or clinical-history corroboration will be shown separately, and missing corroboration will not be described as confirmation. Evidence-supported age-at-event analyses will require reviewer-agreed dates corroborated by contemporaneous, dateable evidence for each event claimed as verified. If a dated after-change video or similar item is the only objective evidence, its first documented post-change date will be reported separately, even when the earlier baseline break or true change date cannot be well supported. Retrospective-only reports will be retained and counted separately, but will not be represented as verified no-anchor timing cases.

No vaccination-to-onset or well-visit-to-onset lag can be computed for this group without an in-window visit, and no artificial visit date will be assigned. Because the study recruits families who already report an autistic child with an identifiable onset, its no-anchor cases do not provide an unvaccinated population incidence rate or a stand-alone estimate of the natural onset rate. Their distributions can be informative descriptively, subject to selection, recall, documentation, and healthcare-use differences.

Because the study does not collect complete vaccination histories, it will not describe the primary analysis as a full self-controlled case series and cannot directly model all earlier doses or event-dependent delay or cancellation of later vaccination. Those questions would require a separate data collection or study.

Candidate comparisons—including the calendar-quarter and child age-quarter date-anchoring checks—must have a defined purpose, denominator, eligibility rule, and null model if used inferentially. A raw histogram or similarity to a scheduled age pattern is descriptive and is not a causal test.

### Confirmatory and exploratory results

Every analysis will be labeled as one of the following:

- **Confirmatory:** fully specified and frozen before relevant outcome-linked exposure records are examined.
- **Secondary:** prespecified but not the primary test.
- **Exploratory:** developed after inspecting the data, including unexpected vaccine-specific or subgroup patterns.

Exploratory findings may justify independent confirmation but will not be relabeled as prespecified.

## 9. Data access, retention, and public release

Identifiable contact information and submitted evidence files will be accessible only to personnel authorized under the IRB-approved protocol and required for their study role. Contact information, parent-entered survey responses, uploaded evidence files, and analytic data will be stored separately with role-based access. Additional qualified researchers may receive controlled access to source evidence for independent re-review only through an IRB-approved process and applicable confidentiality and data-use terms; source files will not be made public.

The final protocol must establish one consistent retention schedule. The working approach is to retain all collected study information for 20 years after study closure, unless the IRB or applicable law requires longer retention:

- Retain all parent-entered Survey 1 and Survey 2 content, including original unprompted narratives and partial responses; the coded analytic dataset, abstraction audit trail, protocol, analysis code, and regulatory records.
- Retain all parent-uploaded source evidence, including videos, photographs, vaccination records or screenshots, clinical notes, messages, calendar images, and other documents supplied as proof or corroboration. The previous 18-month evidence-file destruction rule is withdrawn.
- Retain contact information and original restricted records separately from analytic and public-use datasets. Public-use data will use derived ages and intervals rather than direct identifiers or absolute dates. Retaining restricted originals for audit does not make them public.

Source materials will be encrypted from receipt, including protected backups, with documented key custody and recovery, access authorization, audit logging, incident response, and eventual secure destruction procedures. These controls and the roles permitted to access identifiable materials will be specified before IRB submission. Consent will state clearly that all submitted survey content and evidence files are planned to be retained for 20 years after study closure and that submitted information cannot be withdrawn or revoked under the proposed policy. The retention, controlled re-review, and no-revocation terms must be disclosed prominently before submission and approved by the IRB.

The proposed public-use dataset will include a redacted version of each submitted unprompted narrative, including narratives in partial Survey 2 responses from participants who stop or decline at the additional disclosure. These narratives are a central scientific product of the study because they preserve the parent's event sequence before named-event prompts were shown. Public release of narratives from partial responses is conditional on explicit advance consent, IRB approval, and the disclosure-risk review below; if any of those conditions is not met, the restricted original remains retained for permitted study use but is not released publicly.

Before release, trained reviewers will remove or generalize direct identifiers and details that could reasonably identify a child, parent, clinician, clinic, school, employer, location, or other person. Examples include names, initials, exact addresses, contact information, record numbers, exact calendar dates, rare locations, and uniquely identifying personal circumstances. Redaction will preserve event order, relative timing, developmental observations, and medically relevant content whenever that can be done safely. Both the original restricted narrative and its public redacted version will be retained for 20 years as part of the study dataset.

Public release remains subject to IRB approval and a documented disclosure-risk review of the actual proposed file. Direct identifiers, contact information, absolute dates, and uploaded evidence files will not be made public. The consent will state prominently that redacted narratives and coded survey data will be released publicly and cannot be withdrawn after submission under the study's no-revocation policy. The retained source evidence and any more detailed dataset may be made available only to qualified researchers through the approved controlled-access process.

## 13. Interpretation limits

Recruitment through an autism membership organization or clinic is not a probability sample of all families with autistic children. The study can calculate response, eligibility, follow-up, and record-retrieval rates within each documented invitation funnel. It cannot use an organization's membership list or a clinic's patient pool to estimate autism incidence, population prevalence of identifiable-onset developmental change, or population vaccine risk without additional population-based sampling information.

Likewise, a temporal concentration of baseline breaks after vaccination would be an association with the start of a continuous episode, not by itself an association with the first definite autism-related developmental change. Vaccines can be followed by transient fever, tiredness, or distress without causing autism; that possibility must be represented in the baseline-break null model. The separate definite-change histogram addresses a different endpoint. Interpretation of either still depends on selection, record completeness, age and schedule effects, date accuracy, co-occurring illness and medical care, event-dependent vaccination, and valid comparison periods. Because reviewers see vaccination information in their one-pass assessments, even two- or three-reviewer agreement does not rule out a shared influence of that information on dating either event. Causal conclusions would require converging evidence and designs capable of addressing those alternatives.

The 90-day lookback is a deliberate limit on the requested visit records and the corresponding vaccination-lag histograms, not proof that no earlier vaccination occurred. A child whose most recent vaccination was 200 days before the first definite change will be retained and described as having no reported vaccination in the 90-day window, but that earlier vaccination will not be requested as the study's visit anchor or represented in the vaccination-lag histograms. The study therefore cannot characterize the full distribution of time from every child's last pre-change vaccination to the change. It will report the number of cases with no reported vaccination in the window and distinguish parent-reported never-vaccinated children from children known to have been vaccinated earlier and those with unknown history. Any inference about the 0–90-day distribution is conditional on this ascertainment window and the documented-case selection process.
