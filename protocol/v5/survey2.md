# Survey 2: unprompted reconstruction and documented follow-up

**Version:** 1.19 draft
**Date:** October 4, 2026
**Status:** Working instrument for IRB review; not approved for recruitment  
**Controlling design:** [study_overview.md](study_overview.md)

Survey 2 is offered to every authorized possible-case email referral from the partner within the approved recruitment window, irrespective of date or records-confidence. Research consent and applicable legal authority precede answers. Parent estimates, approximate dates and unknowns are retained; the 6–24-month detailed subset and evidence tiers are determined from study-collected answers, not the partner screen. It is displayed **one question at a time**. The original 15-day narrative is completed, saved, and locked before any named candidate event appears. The later disclosure and all consent/retention wording are proposed for IRB review, not approved text.

## Part A: before any named-event disclosure

Initial administration is English-only, including participant information and consent. Participation requires understanding the English materials. Any later translated instrument requires separate review and approval before use.

ONSET is the single date when either parent first noticed the qualifying lasting change, collected in A2. Earlier illness or uncertain functioning remains timeline context. B, asked after disclosure, is the total complete normal-day count between the selected vaccination and ONSET, including disconnected normal periods.

### Which child should you answer about? — instructions, not a screening question

Answer about the youngest autistic child who appeared to develop normally and then had a clear lasting change first noticed at age 6–24 completed months. If none fits that description or you are unsure, answer about your youngest autistic child. Keep answering about that same child. Out-of-range or uncertain answers are retained, not a reason to block Survey 2.

### Question A1 — original unprompted narrative (first substantive question; required)

Think back to the first day when you or the other parent **clearly noticed a lasting change** in your child's behavior or skills. In your own words, tell us what your child was like before that change and what happened **on each of the 15 days leading up to it and on the first clear-change day**, as best you can remember. Describe what you directly observed, what others told you, what changed, who noticed it, and what happened next. Include anything that seemed relevant to you at the time. It is fine to say that a day is not remembered. Please do not consult the later questions while writing this account.

[Long text; required before advancing. Save on submission; preserve the original verbatim text and timestamp; lock it against editing when the participant advances. Do not display named-event suggestions, examples, or prompts on this screen. The interface may supply neutral day labels without inserting events.]

### Question A3 — earlier uncertain-functioning period and return to baseline

Before that first clear change, was there a period when you could not tell whether your child was functioning normally—even if your child later seemed completely back to normal? If there were several such periods, tell us about the **most recent** one before the clear change. [Yes / No / Unsure.]

**If Yes:** About when did that period begin and end? [Dates, approximate dates, or unknown; do not limit the lookback to 15 or 90 days.] What was it like? [Free text without suggested causes.] In your judgment, did your child return **fully** to their prior normal behavior and skills before the clear change? [Yes / No / Unsure.] If Yes, approximately when did that full return begin? [Date / approximate / unknown.] Preserve brief or partial-day returns separately from no full return. These answers describe the timeline without assigning a cause.

This question is cause-neutral. It provides context before ONSET, not a separate onset date or the vaccination-window B count.

## Part C: structured questions after affirmative continuation

The following is respondent-facing question content and branching, not a final screen layout. Each prompt appears separately. All date fields allow “unknown” or approximate answers without forcing a guessed exact date. For ONSET and vaccination separately, ask whether the date is exact or approximate and the earliest/latest plausible dates when known; permit a week/month or unknown range without inventing a day. Verification is assigned during evidence review, not by a parent's confidence alone. Do not prefill uncertain dates from event dates.

1. **Child's date of birth:** What is your child's birthdate? [Month/day/year, parent report; no documentary proof required.] Confirm that this is the child selected under the Survey 2 instructions. The partner supplies no child-level answers to compare or prefill.
    **Country context — separate screens:** In which country was your child living when you first noticed the lasting change (ONSET)? [Country / cannot remember.] If an eligible vaccination is reported below: In which country was your child living at that vaccination? [Country / same as at ONSET / cannot remember.] Ask the vaccination-country screen only after the relevant vaccination has been identified. Record country only; do not request address or city. Keep unknown separate from no applicable vaccination. These describe residence, not proof of the country where a vaccine was administered; do not infer the latter if the child traveled.
2. **Chronological account after disclosure:** In your own words, describe what your child was like in the days immediately before the most recent vaccination before the clear change, what happened on the vaccination day, and the timeline from then until the day you first noticed the lasting behavior or skill change (ONSET). Include **every period when your child was at their usual pre-vaccination behavior-and-skill baseline**, even if nothing notable happened during that period, as well as periods when they were not or you could not assess their functioning. For each period, give approximate dates or duration, what behavior and skills you actually observed, and who observed them. Describe any return to baseline and any later departure, including multiple separate normal periods. These normal periods are the days counted in B; the account should cover the whole vaccination-to-ONSET window, not only symptoms or notable events. Identify days or stretches you cannot remember rather than assuming they were normal or abnormal. Do not guess a date or a cause. If no vaccination occurred in the 90-day window, describe the chronology around the most recent vaccine-free well-child visit, if any, through ONSET. If neither occurred, describe the sequence leading to ONSET without inventing a visit date. [Long text; shown only after Part B disclosure; the prompted timeline may extend beyond the original 15-day narrative.] Preserve this prompted account separately from locked A1; do not use it to overwrite the original account.
3. **What changed on the first clear-change day:** On that day, what behavior, if any, appeared for the first time? [Description / none / unsure.] What skill or behavior that your child had previously demonstrated was reduced or absent that day, if any? [Description / none / unsure.] For each reported change, ask what the prior usual pattern had been, who directly observed the change, and whether it persisted. Record new-behavior-only, loss/reduction-only, both, and unresolved cases separately. Do not equate fever or illness alone with an autism-related developmental change.
4. **Uncertainty clarification:** Were there days between the last definitely normal observation and the first definitely changed observation when the child's usual skills or behaviors could not be assessed? [Yes / No / Unsure.] Record earliest/latest plausible change dates, rather than replacing ONSET with an uncertain-period date. Keep later clarifications separate from the locked A1 account.
5. **Diagnosis status:** Has a professional diagnosed this child with autism? [Yes / No / Evaluation in progress / Unsure.] If Yes, ask approximate date and clinician type, if known. Documentation is welcome but not required to participate; parent report and documented diagnosis must be distinguishable.
6. **Other preceding events:** In the period you described, do you recall illness or fever, medication, anesthesia, a medical encounter, environmental change, or another event you consider relevant? [For each: Yes / No / Unsure; date or duration; short description. Do not imply any answer is preferred.] Later prompted answers never overwrite A1.
7. **Regularly scheduled well-child visit:** Was there a regularly scheduled well-child visit on the first clear-change date or during the 90 calendar days before it? [Yes / No / Unsure.] If Yes, give the most recent such visit date (or approximate date). Were any vaccinations actually given at that visit? [Yes / No / Unsure.] Was there any later vaccination on or before the clear-change date? [Yes / No / Unsure; if Yes, obtain later visit details below.]
8. **Most recent vaccination:** Was any vaccination given on the first clear-change date or during the 90 calendar days before it, including during an earlier illness or period of uncertain functioning? [Yes / No / Unsure.] If Yes, what was the **most recent actual vaccination date** in that window, and which products were given **on that date**? [Date; product names as documented or remembered; “unsure” allowed.] Was another vaccination given after the visit you first identified but on or before the clear-change date? [Yes / No / Unsure; if Yes, update the most recent date/products and preserve the correction.] For a same-day visit, record whether administration was before first notice, after it, or the order cannot be remembered; day-level A=0 alone does not establish intraday order. Do not ask for a full immunization history as a participation requirement.
9. **Condition before vaccination:** If a pre-change vaccination was reported, in the days immediately before it was the child ill or showing anything unusual? [Yes / No / Unsure; if Yes, describe what and approximately when it began.] What behavior and skills did you directly observe when the child was last clearly at their usual level **before that vaccination**? [Brief description / cannot remember or establish.] Immediately before the vaccination was given, was the child at that usual level? [Yes / No / Could not assess / Unsure.] If not clearly Yes, ask when the child was last definitely at that level. Keep physical wellness distinct from developmental baseline; a pre-vaccination symptom or observed departure from usual functioning must not be dated after the vaccination merely because the visit is the chosen anchor.
10. **Previous vaccination spacing:** If a pre-change vaccination was reported, did you understand the child to be following the routine recommended schedule, a delayed/alternative plan, or are you unsure? [Three choices.] Regardless of that answer, when was the immediately **previous vaccination visit on a different date**? [Date if known / approximate interval in days, weeks, or months / no earlier vaccination / don't know.] This spacing answer is parent-reported unless independently supported; multiple products on one date are one visit.
11. **Lifetime vaccination status:** To your knowledge, has the child **ever** received a vaccination? [Yes / No / Unsure.] A No is coded as parent-reported never vaccinated, not independently verified. If no vaccination and no well-child visit occurred in the 90-day window, record both absences and do not force an artificial visit date.
12. **Symptoms after vaccination and total normal days:** If a vaccination was reported, did fever, malaise, unusual crying, lethargy, or another acute symptom occur afterward but before the first clear developmental change? [Yes / No / Unsure.] If Yes, when did the symptoms first begin, and how long after vaccination was it until the last such symptom ended? [Same day / number of days / symptoms had not ended before the clear change / approximate / cannot remember; collect the approximate end date separately when available.] Do not call these symptoms vaccine-caused merely because they followed vaccination.

    **Separately ask B:** Between that vaccination and the day you first noticed the lasting change, how many complete days in total was your child at their normal behavior-and-skill baseline? By normal, we mean the behavior and skills you observed before vaccination, as described in question 9—not merely that fever, crying, or other symptoms had ended. Add normal days from all periods in this window, even if interrupted by non-normal days. Count dates from vaccination day through the day before ONSET, counting vaccination day only if normal throughout that day. Do not include days before vaccination or ONSET day. [Nonnegative integer / approximate / at least this many days / cannot determine / baseline cannot be established. Known valid values are 0 through A, but preserve an entered B>A with a warning and clarification flag rather than preventing its submission or clamping it.] If dates or day classifications are uncertain, preserve that uncertainty rather than forcing a count. Zero means no complete normal days in this window; unknown is not zero. If no eligible vaccination is identified, B is not applicable.

    **B follow-up — non-normal or unassessable days in the window:** What did you observe on days you did not count as normal? [Free text first, then select all applicable: fever / illness / malaise or unusual tiredness / persistent or inconsolable crying / unusual behavior or reduced skills / other / could not assess usual functioning / cannot remember / all days normal.] Give approximate dates or durations and who observed them, if known. Vaccination alone is not an observed abnormality and does not make a day non-normal. Keep could-not-assess days separate from observed non-normal days; do not assume a vaccination caused a symptom.

    **Complete days and partial-day returns:** Count a day in B only if your child was at their usual pre-vaccination behavior-and-skill baseline throughout that calendar day. Were there any days when your child returned to that baseline for only part of the day? [Yes / No / Unsure; if Yes, describe approximate dates and duration.] Record these returns separately, without adding fractions or rounding them into B. B = 0 does not mean there was no brief return to normal. Do not count an overnight period spanning parts of two calendar days as a full calendar day; preserve its description. Throughout-day classification is based on the parent's ordinary observations, not a requirement for continuous observation; keep genuinely unassessable periods uncertain.

    **Day-count clarification:** In this same vaccination-to-ONSET window, how many days can you classify as definitely normal throughout, definitely not normal throughout, or unknown/could not assess? [Counts for each / approximate / cannot determine; separately note unremembered versus unassessable days.] Present each prompt separately. If A is exact, check that these categories account for A days without forcing answers to agree. A day with a definite non-normal portion is not a complete normal day, even if another portion was normal; preserve that partial return. If only incomplete recall or inability to assess prevents classification, retain unknown/unassessable status rather than abnormal status. With unknown/unassessable days, record the definite-normal count as **at least N**, not an exact B. For example, A = 20 with 5 definitely normal, 10 definitely non-normal and 5 unknown means B is at least 5, possibly up to 10. Do not label the unknown days abnormal.

    Preserve the direct B answer, precision, and any supporting day-by-day account separately from A3. Check B against the normal periods described in the prompted chronology; log discrepancies and any clarification separately rather than overwriting either account or forcing agreement. A narrative's silence about symptoms is not evidence that a day was normal. If B exceeds A, flag the discrepancy and seek clarification; never silently clamp the raw answer. A = 0 implies B = 0 under this complete-day convention.
13. **Available dated materials:** What existing materials could help check the timing or description of the change, the uncertain period, the most recent vaccination, or a vaccine-free well-child visit? [Select all available plus free text: medical/therapy/school/daycare record, dated message/email, diary/calendar, lawfully recorded call or contemporaneous call note, photograph/video, visit/immunization record, other, none/unsure.] An after-only video may establish an earliest **documented post-change observation**, not necessarily the day change began. No document proving the child's birthdate is requested.
14. **Records contact and upload:** May we contact you to request the available materials? [Yes / No.] If Yes, explain that the team requests supporting materials in records-confidence phases. A Yes answer does not itself send a request or enable an upload. Present the IRB-approved secure request/upload path and records authorization when that person's records phase is opened. Request the actual date/products of the **most recent** vaccination in the 90-day window; if none, the most recent vaccine-free regular well-child visit in that window; and any available dated onset evidence. When neither visit exists, request only relevant dated onset evidence. Do not ask parents to prove that no vaccination ever occurred. [Payment amount and objective completion rules displayed under approved language before upload.]

## Implementation, retention, and analysis safeguards

- Retain the parent's direct B count/status and timeline separately from three independently locked reviewer B assessments and any exact two-of-three accepted reviewer value/status. Preserve count bounds, precision, evidence citations, conflicts and parent-versus-reviewer differences; never overwrite the parent answer. Review agreement does not by itself establish verification or turn a lower bound into an exact count.
- The narrative is the first substantive question after consent. Show one question at a time; save A1 and A2–A4 before Part B, lock A1, and never show vaccination or other named candidate events before Part B. Track A1 completion, Part B display, affirmative continuation, explicit decline, abandonment, and all partial-response states separately.
- Preserve raw answers and correction history. Do not discard a submitted account merely because it lacks a visit, lacks diagnostic documentation, conflicts with later answers, or does not fit an analysis. Keep referral/contact permission, Survey 2 reported-age/phenotype flags, records-request status, 100-submission administrative count, full-payment eligibility, and final scientific eligibility as **separate flags**.
- B counts all complete normal days in the selected vaccination-to-ONSET window, including disconnected periods, using directly observed pre-vaccination behavior/skills as baseline. Exclude pre-vaccination and ONSET-day observations; known values obey 0 ≤ B ≤ A. Preserve exact, approximate, lower-bound, unknown and baseline-unestablished answers. Keep non-normal versus unassessable days distinct. Without an eligible vaccination, B is not applicable, not zero. Check B against normal periods in the prompted narrative without rewriting raw responses. A3 remains separate timeline context.
- ONSET is the parent-first-notice date, with parent report, reviewer-specific assessment, accepted value and precision kept separately. Earliest documented post-change date is a separate evidence field, not another onset date. No symptom, vaccination or documentation date substitutes for unresolved ONSET.
- The three reviewers make independent one-pass judgments using the narrative and available evidence, blinded to one another's answers. [records_review_manual.md](records_review_manual.md) and [eligibility_and_construction.md](eligibility_and_construction.md) specify exact agreement, evidence tiers and scientific subset rules; this questionnaire does not confer verification on a parent's answer.
- A good-faith materials submission earns the guaranteed $25 under the proposed IRB-approved schedule. Full-response compensation may increase in $25 steps at two-week checkpoints up to $100, with retroactive equalization for equivalent completed work. Payment and invitations never depend on vaccination status, timing, or hypothesis support. The survey must display the **currently active** amount and exact requirements before a participant undertakes the records task.
- All submitted answers, including partial responses, and all uploaded evidence are proposed for encrypted, controlled-access retention for 20 years after study closure. Only appropriately redacted narratives and coded data are proposed for public release; uploaded evidence and direct identifiers stay restricted. Consent, staged disclosure, non-revocation, partial-response use, payment, and release require explicit IRB approval and an operational privacy plan.
- Before deployment, implement and test [survey_screen_map.md](survey_screen_map.md), including acknowledged saving/lock behavior, required-answer-or-unknown fields, accessible date entry, all branches and the dictionary export. Initial administration is English-only; later translations require review/approval. Reconcile every field with the records manual and collection specification, not a nonexistent confirmatory statistical analysis plan. Display the complete approved records authorization and active payment terms from [consent_and_disclosure.md](consent_and_disclosure.md) before upload.
