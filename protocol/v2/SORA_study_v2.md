# CHD Multisite SORA Parent-Survey Data-Resource Study Overview v2.2

## A focused study designed to identify events that may trigger sudden-onset regressive autism (SORA)

## Executive summary

Using a survey link (hosted by our REDCap system) emailed by autism treatment/diagnosis clinics to their clients, we collect temporal information related to SORA onset. The record-level data will be made publicly available to enable independent data analysis. The goal is to identify all significant factors (immunization, medications, fever, illness, geographical move, etc.) that are temporally correlated with SORA onset.

The key features of this study include:

1. Narrowly-defined, rapid-onset case selection based on parent observation
2. Identifying how long ago onset happened (enabling subgroup analysis to compare recent with more distant memories)
3. Identification of child age and sex at onset
4. Identification of significant events before onset (fever, illness, immunization, surgery, medications, etc.)
5. Comparison of two timing models: chronological age at onset versus time since the child's actual most recent vaccination before onset
6. Unaided recall before prompting (describe the sequence of events; what do you think caused the change)
7. Multisite, large N (10 sites with over 5,000 total participants), with 3 types of clinics (pediatric, diagnostic, treatment)
8. Random record-selection follow-up to assess answer quality against medical records
9. Tracking reasons for drop-out
10. Mixed-view study team
11. Public record-level dataset created from survey results

## Why this study is needed

The marked increase in autism prevalence over recent decades remains without an accepted explanation. Most existing autism studies were not designed to detect short-term triggers around a clearly defined transition: they group together children with very different developmental trajectories and rely on broad exposure windows.

Some studies that conclude vaccines do not cause autism compare children with all vaccines against children with one fewer vaccine and do not verify vaccination status with the parent. Other studies rely on the date of official diagnosis rather than the date the parent first noticed regression, which can lag onset substantially and vary with access and parental persistence. Studies comparing vaccinated with unvaccinated cohorts can also be complicated by the healthy vaccinee effect.

This study is designed to avoid these limitations by anchoring directly to parent-observed onset and by measuring the timing of candidate exposures relative to that onset.

## The goal of the study

The goal is to identify what precedes the sudden onset of autistic regression in a child who was previously developing normally. 

Our mission is to identify all events occurring shortly before SORA onset that occur more often than chance would predict. Finding the SORA triggers will enable us to make changes to reduce autism rates.

Our survey will also make the breakdown of developmental patterns available to researchers, quantifying for the first time at scale:

1. Never met milestones
2. Met milestones, followed by a rapid regression
3. Met milestones, then slow regression
4. Met milestones, then plateaued

And we will ask parents about the type of changes noticed:

1. The child acquired new pathological behaviors (head banging, hand flapping, arching back, etc.)
2. The child lost existing behaviors (e.g., eye contact, social interaction, etc.)
3. The child lost existing skills (e.g., vocabulary, motor skills, etc.)
4. The child acquired new heightened or lowered sensitivities (light, sound, touch, taste/food, pain, etc.)

## A genuinely different approach: the focus on rapid-onset cases (SORA)

This study focuses specifically on rapid-onset autism: cases in which parents can identify a narrow transition date from the child's prior developmental state to a marked regression subsequently confirmed as ASD.

Anchoring questions to the onset date creates a much tighter observation window than conventional autism research. The central focus is on the days immediately before onset.

## SORA definition

**SORA: Sudden-Onset Regression or Alteration associated with autism**

SORA is a developmental change in which the first clear and substantial departure from the child's prior baseline occurs within five days or less, persists for at least four weeks, and consists of at least one of the following:

* A substantial loss of an established behavior or skill;
* The emergence of a new repetitive or self-injurious behavior; or
* A marked change in sensory reactivity.

## How the study works: email survey of autism clinic parents

We will recruit 10 autism clinics, each serving more than 1,000 children with autism. Our preference is for large, multi-doctor autism diagnosis clinics. We will do subgroup analyses comparing recent vs. distant recall to assess whether these extraordinary events fade with time.

Each clinic will email eligible families — all families, not just currently active ones — a link to the parent survey. Families will complete one survey per child. The study team will assemble the responses into a structured public, record-level data resource.

## How the survey protects against prompted recall

The survey order is intentional:

1. The email is sent to all parents of the clinic.
2. Parents are asked to fill out one per child with autism.
3. Parents are told who the sponsor is, who is involved, and why it is important to participate.
4. Parents may opt out (with free-text reason).
5. Parents identify their clinic name.
6. Parents choose the regression pattern that fits their child.
7. If not a SORA pattern, we capture the age autism was first noticed and vaccination status *before that date* (full / partial or delayed / none).
8. The remaining questions are only for SORA-classified children.
9. All SORA parents receive a $10 Amazon gift card upon completion.
10. **Onset anchor:** Parents report the child's age at onset and identify the onset date.
11. **Unaided recall:** Before seeing any checklist, parents describe in their own words what happened before and after onset.
12. **Parent assessment:** Parents report their own causal beliefs *before* detailed prompts.
13. **Five-day event window:** Checklist of events (plausible causes + negative controls like dental visits).
14. **Pre-onset vaccination timing:** Date of the most recent vaccination visit before onset, vaccines given at that visit, the child's age at vaccination, days from that visit to onset, source of the vaccination date, and confidence in the interval. The preceding vaccination visit is also collected to help characterize the child's actual inter-vaccination interval and vaccination pattern.
15. **Vaccination schedule:** Schedule status before onset and whether the schedule continued unchanged, became delayed, or stopped after onset, with the reason collected when known. Post-onset vaccination identity and timing are not used as a null comparison.
16. Email and contact consent for follow-up/record verification.

## Primary temporal hypothesis: chronological age versus time since vaccination

The primary analysis distinguishes two possible clocks for SORA onset:

1. **Chronological-age clock:** Onset follows an age-dependent developmental or biological susceptibility pattern. If this explains the timing, onset should remain anchored to the child's age even when children receive the same vaccine at different ages.
2. **Vaccination-event clock:** Onset risk changes with elapsed time since the child's actual vaccination. If this contributes to the timing, onset should align more closely with each child's vaccination date than with chronological age alone, including among children vaccinated early, late, or on a catch-up schedule.

**The null hypothesis:** SORA onset timing is explained by the child's chronological age and other prespecified background time effects. After flexibly accounting for age, time since the child's actual most recent vaccination before onset provides no additional information about onset timing.

**The prediction:** If vaccination is temporally associated with SORA onset, adding time since the child's actual vaccination will reveal a prespecified short-latency excess that is not explained by the age-at-onset distribution alone. Children vaccinated at different ages would show onset concentrated within a similar period after their actual vaccination visits rather than at the same chronological age.

The primary analysis will compare an age-anchored model with a model that additionally includes time since actual vaccination. It will use actual reported or verified vaccination dates rather than infer exposure from the recommended schedule. The validation pilot will determine what onset-date resolution the data can support; the primary risk window, age model, inclusion rules, and sensitivity analyses will then be prespecified before the main study responses are examined. Analyses will separately report record-verified vaccination dates and will examine whether results differ by vaccination-date source, timing confidence, routine versus delayed or catch-up vaccination, causal belief, and recall recency.

This comparison requires meaningful variation in actual vaccination age. If most vaccinations occur at nearly the same age, chronological age and time since vaccination may be too highly correlated to distinguish reliably. A vaccination-relative pattern would demonstrate a temporal association, not by itself establish that vaccination caused the developmental change.

## Validation pilot and record verification

Before launching, we will run a **validation pilot** to compare parent-recalled onset against independent evidence (clinic notes, early-intervention records, dated family records, or blind-coded video where available) and choose the primary risk window empirically. 

For a consenting subset (~20–30% of SORA cases), we will verify vaccination dates against medical records. **This subset will be selected from respondents who consent to follow-up, with a target of 20–30% of SORA cases.** We will report the verified subset separately to demonstrate that the signal holds when verified by documentation.

## Negative controls

We include carefully chosen negative-control events: vaccine-free well-child visits, dental visits, holidays, and birthdays. If onset clusters around vaccination dates but not around these controls, that separates triggering from anchoring or ascertainment.

## Data handling and privacy

- We release **relative timing** (e.g., day −3, day −1) rather than calendar dates in the public file.
- A controlled-access version with more granular data will be available under a data-use agreement for qualified researchers.
- Email addresses and recontact linkage are stored separately from analytic data.
- IRB, HIPAA, and legal review will be resolved before recruitment.

## What this study can and cannot establish

**What it can do:**
- Describe what parents of SORA children remember and believe;
- Test whether time since the child's actual most recent vaccination provides information about onset timing beyond a flexible chronological-age model, with record verification in a subset;
- Assess how reports vary with causal belief and recall recency.

**What it cannot do:**
- Estimate population-level autism risk by vaccination status (requires the companion clinic-level count);
- Estimate the population prevalence of regression subtypes from a self-selected case survey;
- Eliminate all unmeasured confounding between vaccinated and unvaccinated families.

## Previous similar work: the Goldberg study

The [Goldberg study](https://cpb-us-e2.wpmucdn.com/faculty.sites.uci.edu/dist/c/362/files/2018/05/langregression.pdf) (2003) is the primary precedent for this work. Goldberg enrolled 132 children with ASD and identified 44 who experienced early regression, typically at 18–21 months.

Goldberg asked about 17 possible associations concurrent with onset, and vaccination was the most frequently identified event:

* Immunizations reported as concurrent with loss: **67.6%**
* Illness: **39.5%**
* Medication: **33.3%**
* Parents identifying one event associated with regression: **52.3%**
* Of those, **52.2% identified immunization** (alone or with fever/seizure/medication).

However, these results were aggregate and did not provide granular data (individual responses, dates, vaccine types, time intervals). Goldberg did not distinguish whether vaccination occurred before or after onset, and relied on retrospective parental recall without the record-level verification or negative-control events included in our protocol. Goldberg's home-video work validated that regression occurred, but did not establish the precision of day-level onset dating — which is why our design includes a validation pilot and record-level verification.

## Why this study is hard to dismiss

- Critics cannot say "we need to see the raw data" — it is public.
- Critics cannot say "the analysis cherry-picked" — anyone can re-analyze it.
- Critics cannot say "the null was chosen after seeing the data" — it is pre-registered.
- Critics cannot say "the subset was manipulated" — the verification protocol is pre-registered, and the verified subset is reported separately.
